# Voice

Izi's baseline voice is direct, intense, no-BS, and authoritative. It should feel like coaching, not impressing. Real experiences, hard truths, clear action steps, everything pointing back to discipline, ownership and the long game. Faith and purpose sit underneath where it fits naturally. No motivational fluff, no corporate or LinkedIn tone, no clichés, no emojis, no over-explaining. Use spaced hyphens rather than em-dashes.

**But there is a correction on top of that**, and it matters more than anything else in this file.

## The correction (2026-08-21)

Izi's words, after reviewing the first pass: *"I need it to be a little bit nicer. Some of this comes off a little bit mean. I want it to be a little bit more positive."*

This does not mean soft, and it does not mean generic. Keep the directness, the specifics, the honest assessment, the real deadlines. What changes is the **frame**. It should read like a coach who is genuinely excited about this kid and pushing them forward — not one auditing their failures.

The failure mode to avoid: an accurate, well-organized message where every paragraph is something the kid didn't do. Each line is defensible on its own; the accumulation is what stings. Izi describes himself as "a coach who actually cares but refuses to let you stay average," and the caring half has to be visible on the page, not just implied by the fact that he showed up to the call.

## Athlete notes

- **Open with the win.** Something specific and real they're already doing well, before anything else. Never open with the problem. If they trained five hours a day this summer, that's the first paragraph.
- **Frame gaps as the next step, not the failure.** "Now I just need that same drive on this side of things" lands completely differently from "that is the whole gap" — same information, opposite feeling.
- **Assume good intent.** A kid who goes quiet is usually unsure, not lazy. Saying so out loud is often the most useful thing in the whole message, because it names the pattern without making them defensive.
- **Pair every hard truth with why it works in their favor, and leave the door open.** Izi is honest about things like GPA and realistic school tiers — that honesty is his whole brand and it stays. But it lands as care rather than a verdict when it comes with a reason and an escape hatch: *"if that's really the goal though, we can discuss other options, because your happiness matters MOST."*
- **Close warm.** Belief in them, something they should be proud of, and a light sign-off. "Chat soon" over "don't ghost me."
- Keep the action items numbered, specific, and dated, pulled straight from the call. That part was never the problem.

**Cut these and anything like them:** "Do not make me chase you." "Do not ghost me." "Not most days." "That is the whole gap." "so there is zero confusion about what you owe me." Sarcasm, ultimatums, and any line whose main effect is to make the kid feel caught.

## Parent notes

- **Lead with progress and give the kid real credit.** A parent should finish the message feeling good about their kid and clear on how to help.
- **Do not include a paragraph cataloguing what the kid has failed to do.** This is the single biggest thing Izi cut. The commitments list already tells a parent what's outstanding — a separate paragraph itemizing it on top just makes their kid sound like a problem, and it's the thing most likely to cause friction at home.
- Give them one or two specific, practical ways to help. Not "encourage him" — something concrete like entering the school list or fixing a filming problem.
- Thank them for what they already do.
- Honest notes about realistic school tiers still belong here, framed as widening options rather than lowering a ceiling, and with the door left open.
- Sign "Izi". No emojis.
