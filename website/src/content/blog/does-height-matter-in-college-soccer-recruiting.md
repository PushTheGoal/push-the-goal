Every parent of a smaller athlete has done the math in the stands. The other keeper is three inches taller. The center back on the other team looks like a sophomore in college. And the question shows up on the drive home: does height matter in college soccer recruiting?

Yes, at a few positions. Much less at the rest. And at every position, it matters less than what your athlete does with the height they have.

## Where height matters most

**Goalkeepers.** This is the position where size gets talked about most, because reach covers the top corners and the crosses. Coaches watching a keeper want to know one thing first: can this athlete control the air in their own box?

**Center backs.** Defending crosses and set pieces is a big part of the job, and coaches look for athletes who win their headers.

**Target forwards.** Some systems want a forward who holds the ball up and wins balls in the air. Not every team plays that way, which is why this one depends on the program.

## Where it matters less

Midfield, outside backs and wingers. At these positions coaches watch for speed of decision, first touch under pressure, work rate and how an athlete reads the game. A quick, sharp midfielder who sees the pass early gets recruited at every level.

Look at any college roster and you will find plenty of athletes nobody would call big. They are fast, technical and hard to play against, and that is what got them there.

## What coaches weigh instead

**Speed of play.** The biggest gap between good high school players and college rosters is how fast an athlete decides. Height does not help a player who takes three touches when one would do.

**Timing in the air.** Winning a header comes down to timing, positioning and the willingness to go up for it. Plenty of smaller defenders win their duels because they read the ball first.

**Strength and athleticism.** A college game is physical. An athlete who is strong on the ball, quick off the mark and fit in the 80th minute closes a lot of the size gap.

**Everything off the field.** Grades, coachability, how the athlete communicates with coaches. I go through the full list in my post on [what college soccer coaches look for](/what-college-soccer-coaches-look-for/).

## If your athlete is on the smaller side

**Build film that answers the question before a coach asks it.** A shorter keeper needs clips of high balls claimed, crosses punched clear and full-stretch saves to the top corner. A smaller center back needs clips of headers won and duels won through timing. I tell my keepers this straight. On a call with one of them I put it plainly: "You're on the shorter side, right? So we got to make sure that you can dive high." Then we built the work around it. My guide to [making a recruiting video coaches watch](/how-to-make-a-soccer-recruiting-video/) covers how to cut those clips.

**List the real height on the profile.** Coaches see your athlete in person at camps and showcases. An inflated number costs trust the moment they do. Put the true height on the [recruiting profile](/soccer-recruiting-profile/) and let the film make the case.

**Put the gym on the calendar.** Strength, power and agility work makes a smaller athlete harder to move and quicker to the ball. It shows up in the 80th minute and on film.

**Cast the list wide.** Programs play different styles, and different styles want different players. A school that plays out of the back through midfield may care far more about your athlete's touch than their height. Spread the target list [across every level](/d1-vs-d2-vs-d3-vs-naia-college-soccer/) and the right fit is far more likely to be on it.

**Remember who is still growing.** Many high school athletes are not done growing, and coaches who recruit freshmen and sophomores know it. A sophomore keeper with great hands and footwork is a real prospect.

## If your athlete is tall

Size opens doors at the positions above. It does not keep them open. Coaches will still ask whether the athlete moves well, plays with the ball at their feet and makes good decisions. Tall keepers who cannot get down low and tall defenders who turn slowly get exposed at the next level. The work list is the same: technique, speed and film that shows both.

## FAQ

**Does height matter in college soccer recruiting?**
At goalkeeper and center back it is one of the things coaches look at. At most other positions, speed of decision, first touch, athleticism and work rate matter more.

**Can a short goalkeeper play college soccer?**
Yes. A smaller keeper needs film that shows command of the box: high balls claimed, crosses dealt with and saves at full stretch. Footwork and distribution help too.

**Should my athlete list their height on the recruiting profile?**
Yes, and accurately. Coaches see athletes in person at camps and showcases, and a true number keeps their trust.

**Does height matter more at the higher levels?**
It depends on the program more than the level. Some teams want size at certain positions and others build around technical players. A wide target list across levels finds the programs that fit your athlete.
