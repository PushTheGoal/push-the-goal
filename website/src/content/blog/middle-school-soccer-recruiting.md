The email arrives in seventh grade. An "elite" program with a college pathway, a national showcase your twelve-year-old has been "selected" for, a recruiting profile with a monthly fee. It all points at the same fear: if you do not do this now, your kid falls behind.

We talk to the families on the other end of that fear every week, once the kids are in high school. So here is a sorting of what a middle schooler's soccer life does and does not do for college recruiting later, from people who see how it plays out.

## What does not matter (as much as you are being told)

**The name of the club at eleven.** Coaches recruit high school players. What club your athlete was on in sixth grade never comes up. The club that matters is the one they play for at sixteen, and the [answer to whether that has to be ECNL or MLS Next](/do-you-need-ecnl-or-mls-next-to-get-recruited/) is more open than the sales pitch suggests. Choose the middle school club on coaching, playing time and drive time. Nothing else.

**Rankings.** Youth rankings measure which team won a tournament of eleven-year-olds. They do not predict who plays in college. Most athletes who play in college were not on a top-ranked team at twelve, and many who were are out of the game by fifteen.

**Showcases and "exposure" events before high school.** Exposure to whom? College coaches are not evaluating seventh graders and in most cases cannot contact them. An event that sells exposure to a middle schooler is selling the word. [ID camps have a place](/are-soccer-id-camps-worth-it/), and that place is high school.

**Recruiting profiles and highlight videos.** There is no coach who can act on them yet. A profile started at twelve is a subscription with nobody on the other end.

**Specializing early.** Playing one sport year-round from age ten is linked to more overuse injuries and more burnout, and it does not produce better college players. Izi came back from five knee surgeries. She has opinions about young bodies and rest.

**Winning.** The score of a U12 game has no bearing on anything that happens at seventeen. Development does.

## What matters, and compounds

**Touches and love of the ball.** Unstructured play, futsal, pickup, juggling in the driveway. Technical comfort is built in these years and it is hard to add later. This is also where creativity comes from, and coaches notice creativity.

**Playing time.** A middle schooler on the field for seventy minutes on a mid-table team develops faster than one on the bench of a top team. Every time. Choose minutes.

**Good coaching at the right level.** A coach who teaches, who lets kids make mistakes, and who is not screaming from the sideline. Ask other parents. Watch a training session before you sign.

**Habits.** Sleep, water, schoolwork done without a fight, a short set of touches every day. The athletes who move fast in recruiting at fifteen already had these. They were built somewhere between fifth and eighth grade.

**Independence.** Packing their own bag, talking to their own coach, owning their own schedule. A college coach on a phone call can tell within a minute whether the athlete or the parent runs things. That skill starts now.

**Understanding the road.** Age-appropriate, no pressure: what college soccer is, what the levels are, what coaches will eventually look for. A kid who knows where the road goes trains differently from one who is told to run.

**Still loving it at fourteen.** The single biggest predictor. The most talented ten-year-old in the county is worth nothing to a college coach if they quit in eighth grade.

## A simple test for anything you are offered

Ask one question: **is there a college coach on the other end of this who can do something with it right now?**

If the answer is no, and for a middle schooler it almost always is, then whatever you are being offered is either development or noise. Development is worth paying for: good coaching, playing time, a coach who builds habits. Noise is anything with "elite," "pathway," "exposure" or "recruiting" attached to a fifth through eighth grader.

## What these years are for

Grades 5 through 8 are for building the person who will be recruited later, and for protecting the thing that makes any of it possible, which is a kid who wants to play. ## FAQ

**Does middle school soccer matter for college recruiting?**
The development does. The club name, the rankings and the trophies do not. Coaches recruit high school players, and what a middle schooler builds in habits, technique and love of the game is what shows up then.

**Should my 12-year-old be on an elite or academy team?**
Only if it means better coaching and real playing time. The name on the jersey at twelve does not come up in recruiting. Minutes and development do.

**Are showcases worth it for middle schoolers?**
No. College coaches are not evaluating or contacting middle school players. Showcases and ID camps belong in high school.

**When should my kid start a recruiting profile or highlight video?**
High school, once there is a coach who can act on it. Starting earlier does not create an advantage.

**How do I prepare a middle schooler for college soccer without pressuring them?**
Focus on habits, independence and enjoyment, and give them a coach outside the family to answer to. Keep scholarships and rankings out of the conversation at home.
