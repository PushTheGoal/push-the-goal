An ID camp is the only place your athlete gets coached by the staff that would recruit them, on their field, for a whole day.

## What is a soccer ID camp?

A soccer ID camp is a one or two day camp run by a college program, on its own campus, where the coaching staff trains and evaluates high school players. "ID" stands for identification: the staff is identifying players it might recruit. Your family pays to attend, and your athlete gets hours in front of the coaches who make that program's recruiting decisions.

A typical day: check-in and a bib number, sessions run by the staff, small-sided games, then full games in the afternoon, often with a campus tour or a talk from the coaches about the program. Some camps finish with a short meeting where coaches talk with players they want to follow up with.

It is also, at a lot of schools, a revenue program that will happily take your money and give your kid a nice weekend that changes nothing.

Both versions exist, often at the same university. The difference between them is almost entirely what your family does in the two weeks beforehand.

## When a camp is worth every dollar

**The school is on your athlete's list.** Right level, right academics, right distance, right money. A camp at a school your athlete would attend is research and recruiting at once.

**The coaching staff runs it.** Some camps are staffed mostly by students and outside coaches. Ask directly who is on the field. If the head coach and assistants are not coaching sessions, you are paying for facilities.

**It is small.** Sixty players and four coaches means real evaluation. Three hundred players means your athlete is a bib number.

**Your athlete has emailed first.** This is the whole thing. A coach who knows your athlete's name, position and grad year before the camp watches them differently from a stranger in a group of ninety.

**Your athlete is fit and available.** A camp attended while injured or unfit is money spent on a bad impression.

## When it is expensive hope

The camp is at a school nobody in your family has researched. It was chosen because it is nearby, or because the brochure looked impressive, or because a teammate was going.

Nobody emailed the coach. Your athlete shows up, plays fine, goes home, and waits for a call that was never going to come, because the staff has no idea who they were watching.

That is the version families pay for again and again. The camp is not the problem. The silence around it is.

## The email that changes the outcome

Two to three weeks before, your athlete writes to the coaching staff. Short. Grad year, position, club, GPA, film link, and the line that matters: *I am registered for your camp on the 14th and I am looking forward to working with you.*

Now the coach has a name to attach to a face. Many will look at the film beforehand. Some will make a point of watching your kid in a specific session.

Then, the same day the camp finishes, your athlete sends a thank-you. Thank them for the coaching, mention one thing they learned, say a new highlight reel is coming, and ask the question that keeps the door open: *if there is anything else you would like to see to continue my recruitment, I would love to know what that is.*

That question is the most valuable sentence in this whole post. It converts a camp into a conversation. I lay out the full format in my [guide to emailing college coaches](/how-to-email-college-soccer-coaches/).

## Camps versus showcases

They do different jobs.

A **showcase** puts your athlete in front of many programs at once. Breadth. Best when your list is still wide and you are hunting for interest.

An **ID camp** puts your athlete in front of one program in depth, coached and evaluated up close for hours. Best when a school is already on the list and you want to convert interest into a real relationship.

Most families need both, in that order: showcases to find the schools, camps to close them. A showcase only counts if the right coaches know your athlete is there, so read [how to get college coaches to your athlete's showcase game](/college-soccer-showcase-strategy/) before you register.

## A note for families dealing with an injury

Fall ID camps are one of the best pivots available. They are quieter than the summer circuit, there are fewer players per coach, and an athlete returning from injury gets more individual attention than they would in a July showcase field of three hundred.

If your athlete has missed a window, this is where you go next rather than writing off the year.

## FAQ

**What is a soccer ID camp?**
A camp run by a college soccer program on its own campus, where the coaching staff trains and evaluates high school players it might recruit. Families pay to attend.

**Are college soccer ID camps worth the money?**
They are when the school is on your athlete's list, the coaching staff runs the sessions, the camp is small, and your athlete emails the coach beforehand. Without that last step most camps achieve little.

**Do college coaches recruit from their own ID camps?**
Yes, regularly, but mostly from players they already knew about. The camp confirms an impression more often than it creates one.

**Should we go to a camp at a school we are not interested in?**
No. Spend that money on a school your athlete would attend, or on a showcase where several target programs will be.

**ID camp or showcase, which first?**
Showcases to find schools, camps to deepen the ones that fit.

**What should my athlete send after a camp?**
A same-day thank-you naming something they learned, a note that new film is coming, and a question asking what else the coach would like to see.
