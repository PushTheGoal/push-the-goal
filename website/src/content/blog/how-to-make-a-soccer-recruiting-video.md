Your athlete's highlight video is the first tryout, and most families do not know they are being evaluated before a coach ever replies.

Here is what happens on the coach's end. Your athlete's email lands in an inbox with a few hundred others. The coach clicks the film link, watches for about thirty seconds, and makes one decision: keep watching, or move on. That thirty seconds is the whole audition. Everything below is built around surviving it.

## The mistake that kills most reels in the first ten seconds

The coach cannot tell which player is your athlete.

It sounds small. It ends the evaluation. A coach opens a clip, sees twenty-two players in motion, and has no idea who they are supposed to be watching. If they cannot find your athlete in the opening moments, they do not rewind. They close the tab.

Mark your athlete in the first seconds of every reel. A spotlight circle or an arrow on the opening clips, so the coach knows instantly who they are following. Without a marker, nobody knows whether they are watching your player or the girl in white.

The second reel-killer is the intro. Cinematic titles, a music-video build, a slow logo animation. Every second before the first touch is a second of the coach's attention spent on nothing. Open on soccer.

## What goes in the first reel

Eight to ten clips. That is a real first highlight video, and it is enough.

Families stall for months trying to assemble thirty perfect moments, and the reel never gets made. A tight ten-clip video that exists beats a twenty-clip video that is still "in progress" in November. Getting the clips is the hard part of this whole project, so start pulling them now, from any game film your club records.

Order matters. Strongest clips first, always. A coach who is impressed at clip two will watch clip nine. A coach who has to wait until clip nine for something good never gets there.

Keep the whole thing to three or four minutes.

## What to show, by position

Coaches watch for what the position demands. Pick clips that answer their questions first.

**Goalkeepers.** Shot stopping both ways, high balls claimed, crosses dealt with, one-on-ones, and distribution with hands and feet. Our post on [goalkeeper recruiting](/goalkeeper-recruiting/) goes further.

**Defenders.** One-on-one defending, headers won, recovery runs, and passing out of the back under pressure.

**Midfielders.** Receiving under pressure, turning, switching play, and the pass that breaks a line. Show your athlete scanning before the ball arrives.

**Forwards.** Finishing with both feet, movement off the ball, holding the ball up, and pressing defenders.

Every position: let the assists play through, because the pass is the point.

## How to cut the clips

**Start right as the play begins.** No ten-second run-up while the ball rolls around midfield. Trim to the moment the sequence starts.

**Let assists play through.** When your athlete makes the pass, the pass is the point, so let the clip run to the finish. Coaches want to see vision and decision-making, not only goals.

**Show more than goals.** Defending, first touch under pressure, a switch of play, a keeper's distribution, recovery runs. Coaches recruit complete players, and a highlight reel of nine finishes tells them almost nothing about the other eighty-nine minutes.

**Add captions.** Position, jersey color, and number on screen. Every friction point you remove is one more reason to keep watching.

## Where to put it (and how to link it)

Host the reel on YouTube. Not a file attachment, not a login-walled drive folder, not a platform that asks a coach to create an account. If a coach has to work for the film, the film does not get watched.

Put the YouTube link in the Instagram bio. Put it in every email. Keep everything in one spot so any coach who gets curious about your athlete lands in the same place.

Title the video so it works when a coach searches later: full name, grad year, position, club. "Jordan Ruiz 2028 Center Back Highlights." The description carries the same details plus a contact email.

## The video most families never make

Highlight film proves what your athlete can do. It says nothing about who they are, and that second question is what separates two players with similar clips.

That is the case for a personal statement video. Your athlete on camera, ninety seconds, talking straight to a coach: name, grad year, position, state, how they came to the sport, what their position taught them about communication and pressure, their GPA and course load, what they do outside soccer, and what they want in a college program. Friendly, honest, unscripted enough to sound like a person.

Almost nobody sends one. That is the point. When your athlete's email arrives with a highlight reel and a face and a voice, they stop being a name in a list. It is going the extra mile and getting that personal, and it is what makes an athlete stand out.

Timing tip: late July into August, when coach inboxes thin out, gives a personal statement video far more room to land.

## Your athlete owns this one

One division of labor works better than any other: the athlete owns the film and the social posts, and the recruiting strategy handles the coach emails.

Coaches can tell when a parent made the reel. More importantly, an athlete who cuts their own film starts watching their own game differently, and that shows up on the field. Hand this project to your kid, set a deadline, and check in weekly. Consistency is what produces the result.

Once the film is live, it feeds everything else: the pre-showcase email, the follow-up note, the recruiting profile, the coach who wants a second look before booking a trip. Our [guide to emailing college coaches](/how-to-email-college-soccer-coaches/) shows where the link goes.

## FAQ

**How long should a soccer recruiting video be?**
Three to four minutes. Coaches decide within the first thirty seconds whether to keep watching, so lead with the strongest clips and keep the total tight.

**How many clips should a first highlight reel have?**
Eight to ten. A finished ten-clip reel does more for recruiting than a longer one that never gets sent.

**How do coaches know which player I am in the video?**
Only if you tell them. Put a spotlight circle or arrow on yourself in the opening clips, and add your jersey number and color as an on-screen caption.

**Where should we host a soccer recruiting video?**
YouTube, unlisted or public, linked in every coach email and in your athlete's Instagram bio. Avoid anything that makes a coach log in or request access.

**Do we need a professional videographer?**
No. Club game film cut in a free phone editor like CapCut is what most recruited players use. Coaches are evaluating the player, not the production.

**What is a personal statement video?**
A short video of the athlete introducing themselves to coaches: grad year, position, academics, and what they want in a program. Few athletes send one, which is why it gets responses.
