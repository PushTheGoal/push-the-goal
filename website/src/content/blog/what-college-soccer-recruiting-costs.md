By the time an athlete is a junior, most soccer families have spent more on this than on any vehicle they own. Club fees, tournaments, hotels, flights, gas, training, camps, gear, and whatever service somebody recommended in a Facebook group.

Then a coach does not answer an email and the family wonders what all of it bought.

Some of that spending does real work in recruiting. A lot of it does none. The difference is not obvious from inside it.

## What families are paying for

Club fees. Tournament and showcase entry. Travel, which is usually the biggest line and the one nobody budgets. ID camps. Private training. Recruiting services and video editing. Equipment.

Roughly none of that is optional-feeling in the moment. Every line arrives attached to a reason your athlete will fall behind without it.

## What earns its money

**Playing at a level where your athlete is challenged and plays.** The core spend, and the one worth protecting. An athlete needs real minutes against real competition. Note both halves: a team where your athlete sits on the bench at a high level develops them less than a team where they play every minute a level down.

**ID camps at schools already on the target list.** The highest-return line in most budgets. Hours of direct evaluation by a staff who could recruit your athlete. The word that matters is *target*. A camp at a school your athlete would never attend is a training session with a logo on it.

**Getting film made.** Whatever it takes to have clips. Izi's standard is eight to ten clips for a first reel, cut tight, with your athlete marked in the opening seconds. Families can do this themselves with a phone and CapCut. Paying somebody is fine too, and either way it is money well spent because nothing else in recruiting works without film.

**Travel to events where coaches from the target list will be.** Which requires knowing who is attending before you book. Ask the event. Ask the club. Then email those coaches before you go.

## What usually does not

**Tournaments with no college coaches present.** Good for the team and for development. Not recruiting spend, and families count it as recruiting spend constantly.

**Showcases nobody was told your athlete was attending.** A showcase does not deliver coaches to your athlete's field. Outreach beforehand does. The event without the emails is an expensive weekend of soccer. This is where most recruiting money quietly disappears.

**Services that promise exposure.** The category, broadly. Blasting a profile to hundreds of coaches produces the same result as any mass email: almost nothing. Coaches respond to a specific message from an athlete who knows their program. Your athlete can send that for free.

**Camps chosen for the brand name.** A camp at a program that will never recruit your athlete is a nice experience. Pick camps off the target list.

**More private training when the film does not exist.** Skill work has value. An athlete with excellent touch and no reel is invisible.

## Spending a small budget well

If the budget is tight, and for most families it is, this is the order.

1. Play somewhere your athlete gets real minutes.
2. Get film made. Free is fine.
3. Send emails. Free.
4. Follow up. Also free.
5. One or two ID camps a year, at target schools.
6. Travel to events where you have confirmed target coaches will be, after emailing them.

Steps two through four are the whole engine of recruiting and cost nothing but hours. Families who work them out-recruit families spending several times more on tournaments and services.

## The uncomfortable part

Money does not buy recruiting. Families with large budgets and no strategy get outworked by families with small budgets and a plan, and Izi watches it happen every season.

The athlete who sends fifty specific emails and follows up four times gets further than the athlete whose family paid for a service and waited. Follow-up is where it turns: coaches respond on the third and fourth attempt, when almost everyone else has stopped.

That is the good news in a post about cost. The part that decides the outcome is the part that is free.

## FAQ

**How much does college soccer recruiting cost?**
It varies enormously by region and club. The bigger question is allocation, since the parts that most affect outcomes, film and outreach and follow-up, cost almost nothing.

**Are showcases worth the money?**
Only when coaches from your athlete's target list are attending and have been emailed beforehand. Without that outreach, a showcase is an expensive tournament.

**Are recruiting services worth paying for?**
Mass exposure to hundreds of coaches gets treated like any mass email. Coaches respond to specific messages from athletes who know their program, and your athlete can send those without paying anyone.

**Do we need to pay someone to make a highlight video?**
No. A phone and a free editing app are enough. Eight to ten clips, cut tight, with your athlete marked in the first seconds.

**What is the best use of a small recruiting budget?**
Real playing time, film, emails, follow-up, then one or two ID camps at schools already on the target list.
