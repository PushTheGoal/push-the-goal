Every soccer parent has heard a version of "grades matter" and filed it next to eat your vegetables. Useful in general, not urgent this week.

Here is the specific version, which is more useful. Grades decide how many programs are able to recruit your athlete at all. Before a coach evaluates a single touch.

## What a coach does with a transcript

Coaches are not admiring the honor roll. They are running two calculations.

**Can this athlete get in?** Every school has an academic floor, and it varies wildly. An athlete a coach loves who cannot be admitted is a dead end, and coaches learn to spot that fast. At academically selective schools, coaches have a limited number of admissions exceptions and spend them carefully.

**What will this athlete cost me?** An athlete with strong grades brings academic money, which means less of the coach's limited athletic aid. That makes them cheaper to sign, and cheaper to sign means more attractive.

So a transcript works as a filter and as a discount at the same time. Neither has anything to do with how good your athlete is at soccer.

## The number families track is the wrong one

An athlete's overall high school GPA and their NCAA core-course GPA are different figures.

The NCAA counts a specific list of approved core courses. Electives that lift a transcript do not count toward it. Which is how a family arrives at senior year proud of a solid GPA and discovers their athlete is short on the number that governs eligibility.

Pull the high school's approved course list from the Eligibility Center site and check your athlete's schedule against it. Do it every year, with the counselor, starting in ninth grade. Rules on minimums have changed recently, so get current requirements from eligibilitycenter.org rather than from anyone's memory.

## Where grades change the recruiting itself

**They widen the list.** A stronger transcript adds academically selective schools, which include a large number of strong soccer programs. A weaker one removes them regardless of ability.

**They speed up responses.** Coaches at selective schools screen academically first. An athlete whose email includes a strong GPA gets read differently than one who left it out.

**They protect the commitment.** Offers get pulled over transcripts more often than over soccer. A senior-year slide is a real way to lose a spot.

**They matter more the further down the levels you go.** Division III has no athletic money, so academic and need-based aid carry the whole package. At D3, grades are the financial aid conversation.

## What to put in a coach email

GPA, and test scores if your athlete has them and the schools want them. One line, near the top, with the grad year and position.

Coaches read it. An email without academic information makes a coach do extra work to find out whether an athlete is even possible for their school, and busy people skip the ones that require extra work.

## If the transcript is already weak

It is fixable, and the fix depends on when you start.

**Freshman or sophomore.** Straightforward. Change the trajectory now and the picture at graduation looks different. Sit with the counselor and build a schedule of approved core courses.

**Junior.** Tighter and still live. A strong junior year moves a GPA meaningfully, and coaches read an upward trend as a real signal about a kid.

**Senior.** Limited room, and the target list changes rather than the transcript. If the grades are low enough to close most four-year doors, my advice is the same every time: go two-year first. [Junior college](/juco-soccer-recruiting/) has no academic barrier to get in, your athlete plays college soccer right away, and two years of good academic work there can make them eligible for schools that would not have looked at their high school record. I took that route myself.

Whatever the timing, be straight with coaches. An athlete who says "my sophomore year was bad, here is what I did about it" is far more compelling than one who hopes nobody looks closely.

## The one-line version

Your athlete's soccer decides which programs want them. Their transcript decides which of those programs can have them, and what it costs your family when they do.

Both are the job.

## FAQ

**Do grades matter for college soccer recruiting?**
Yes, in two ways. They determine which schools can admit your athlete, and they bring academic money that reduces what a coach spends from limited athletic aid.

**What GPA does my athlete need for college soccer?**
It varies by division and by school, and the NCAA requirements have changed recently. Get current figures from eligibilitycenter.org and ask each school directly.

**Is the NCAA GPA the same as my athlete's school GPA?**
No. The NCAA calculates a separate GPA from an approved list of core courses, so the two numbers are often different.

**Should my athlete put their GPA in a coach email?**
Yes, near the top with grad year and position. Coaches screen for it, and leaving it out makes a coach work harder to consider your athlete.

**Can a soccer offer be pulled over grades?**
Yes, and it happens more often than offers pulled over performance. Senior year grades still count.

**What if my athlete's grades are too low for a four-year school?**
Start at a junior college. There is no academic barrier to get in, your athlete plays college soccer right away, and strong grades there open four-year programs later.
