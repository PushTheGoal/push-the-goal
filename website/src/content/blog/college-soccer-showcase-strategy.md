Families pay for the showcase, drive six hours, sit through three games in the heat, and go home having been watched by nobody. Then they book the next one and do it again.

The event does not bring coaches to your athlete's field. It brings coaches to a complex with forty fields on it. Which one they stand at is decided in the two weeks before, by email.

## The math nobody explains

A showcase publishes an attending-coach list with a hundred and fifty programs on it. That list is why families sign up.

Those coaches arrive with a list of names. They have games mapped out, athletes they came to see, and time for a fraction of what is happening. A coach standing at your athlete's field is there because somebody told them to be.

I have an athlete who went from around nine coaches at one showcase game to around twenty-five at the next. Same player, same level, same event format. The difference was outreach done before the second one, including a Division I coach who texted ahead to say they would try to make it.

That gap is the whole post.

## Before: the part that does the work

**Get the attending list.** From the event site, or by asking the tournament or your club director. If a showcase will not tell you which coaches are coming, question whether it is worth the entry fee.

**Cross it against the target list.** Your athlete is not emailing all hundred and fifty. They are emailing the ones on their list, which is why the list needs to exist first.

**Write the emails two to three weeks out.** I draft these with athletes in a shared document, and the athlete sends them. Every school attending that is on the list gets one.

**What goes in it:**
- Name, grad year, position, club and high school, GPA
- The event name
- Team name and the color they play in
- Every game: date, time, field number
- Jersey number
- A film link
- One line on why that program

Field number and jersey number are the two details families leave out and they are the two that decide whether being watched happens. A coach with twenty minutes and no field number goes to a game they can find.

**Follow up two or three days before** with any schedule changes. Fields move constantly. My line for this: "Hey coach, I just want to make sure you had eyes on this. I would hate for you to go to the wrong field." Nothing is being asked for. It is helpful, it is unarguable, and it puts your athlete back on top of the inbox the week it matters.

## During

**Your athlete plays their game.** Not a trial. Coaches evaluating a player are watching decisions, work rate off the ball, and what happens after a mistake. An athlete trying to produce a highlight reel in real time looks worse than one playing normally.

**Warm up like someone is watching**, because someone is. Coaches show up early and evaluate the warm-up more than families think.

**Watch the touchline behavior.** How your athlete talks to teammates and referees, and what they do when they come off. All of it is being read.

**Parents stay quiet.** A coach who hears a parent working the referee for ninety minutes has learned something about the family they would be recruiting. This costs athletes opportunities every season.

## After

**Same day.** Every coach who was there gets an email that night. Thank them for coming, and use my closing question: "If there is anything else you would like to see to continue my recruitment process, I would love to know what that is."

That question does two jobs. It invites a reply, and if the reply comes it gives your athlete an assignment, which is the beginning of a real relationship.

**Email the ones who did not come too.** Short, warm, no guilt. Here is how the weekend went, here is new film, here is where I will be next. Silence is usually a schedule problem.

**Then follow up again in two to three weeks.** Coaches respond on the third and fourth contact. The showcase is one touch in a sequence, and treating it as a standalone event is how families end up with nothing to show for the entry fee.

## What this means for choosing events

Once you understand that the outreach does the work, you pick events differently.

A smaller showcase with fifteen target-list coaches attending beats a huge one where none of the hundred and fifty are schools your athlete wants. Choose events by who is coming, not by size or reputation.

And if your family can only afford two events a year, spend the money on the two where your athlete's target coaches will be, then do the outreach properly for both.

## FAQ

**How do I get college coaches to watch my athlete at a showcase?**
Email them two to three weeks beforehand with the team, jersey number, and every game's date, time and field number, then follow up a few days before with any changes.

**Are soccer showcases worth the money?**
Only when target-list coaches are attending and have been contacted. Without the outreach beforehand, a showcase is an expensive tournament.

**What should be in a pre-showcase email to a coach?**
Grad year, position, club, GPA, event name, team and colors, jersey number, every game with field and time, a film link, and one line on why that program.

**Should my athlete email coaches who did not show up?**
Yes, warmly and with no guilt. Send a short recap, new film and their next event. Coaches miss games for scheduling reasons, not as a verdict.

**How do we choose which showcases to attend?**
By which coaches from your athlete's target list are attending. A small event with the right coaches beats a large one with none.
