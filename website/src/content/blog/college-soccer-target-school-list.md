Ask a junior where they want to play and you will hear four schools. Three of them are programs that recruit nationally and have their class filled, and one is where their cousin went.

That is not a target list. That is a wish, and it is the reason most families spend a year emailing into silence.

## The number

Izi builds lists of fifty to a hundred schools with her athletes.

Parents flinch at that. It sounds like spray and pray, the opposite of the personal outreach that works. It is neither, and here is why the number has to be that big.

Every program has a roster with a fixed number of spots. In any given year a coach might need two defenders and no keepers. Your athlete can be exactly what a program wants and still get nothing back, because that program is not recruiting their position in their class. Multiply that across levels, geography, budget, and the schools that will not have your athlete's major, and a list of ten produces almost nothing.

A wide list is how you find the programs that have both a need and a fit at the same time. You cannot know which ones those are from the outside. You find out by asking.

## The five filters

**Level.** Be honest, and go wider than one level. Izi's rule from her own calls is that any coach relationship is worth having, because coaches move between schools and levels constantly. A D3 assistant this year is a D1 assistant in two years, and they remember the athletes who were straightforward with them.

Put schools on the list above where your athlete sits today, at their level, and below it. The stretch schools keep the ceiling high. The realistic ones are where the offers come from.

**Major.** If your athlete wants nursing or engineering, cross off every school that does not have it. This filter alone will cut a list fast, and it prevents the worst outcome in recruiting, which is an athlete who transfers after a year because the degree was never there.

**Cost.** Look at what the school costs your family after aid, not the sticker price. Small private schools with strong academic money often cost less than a state school. If a family has military benefits or Yellow Ribbon eligibility, that reshapes the whole list.

**Distance.** How far is too far, and be truthful about it. Some families will drive eight hours for a game and some will not. An athlete who is miserable four states from home does not last, and coaches would rather know early.

**Camps and clinics.** Which of these schools run ID camps your athlete could attend? A camp at a target school is the shortest distance between your athlete and a coach who is evaluating them in person.

## Sorting the list

Three buckets, and be honest about which is which.

**Reach.** Programs where your athlete would be at the edge of what they recruit. Keep some. Do not build a list from them.

**Target.** Programs where your athlete looks like the players already on the roster. This is where the list should be heaviest, and where most offers come from.

**Likely.** Programs where your athlete would be one of the stronger recruits in the class. These are the schools that answer fastest, and they are worth real attention rather than being treated as a backup plan.

A useful check on any school: pull up the current roster. What positions are in each class? Who graduates in your athlete's entry year? A program losing three center backs the year your center back arrives is a much better target than the one that signed two last year.

## Working the list

The list is only useful if it turns into outreach. Send emails with grad year, position, club, GPA and a film link, and say why that specific program. Then follow up, and keep following up.

The families who see results are the ones on the third and fourth round of follow-ups. Coaches respond to the athlete who is still there in November after emailing in August. That persistence reads as genuine interest, and coaches have said as much.

Keep the list in one document with a column for who you emailed, when, and what came back. Recruiting falls apart in the tracking more than in the writing.

## Reworking it

The list is not fixed. It changes when your athlete's film improves, when a season goes well or badly, and when responses come back.

If a level is not answering at all after several rounds, that is information. Add schools a level down. If three programs at a level respond quickly, add more like them. The list should get sharper every few months, not sit in a folder.

## FAQ

**How many schools should be on a college soccer target list?**
Fifty to a hundred. It sounds like a lot until you account for programs that are not recruiting your athlete's position in their class, which you cannot know from the outside.

**Should my athlete only email schools they would attend?**
Every school on the list should be one your athlete would seriously consider, but the bar for "seriously consider" should be lower than families think. Coaches move schools, and relationships carry over.

**How do I know if a program has room for my athlete?**
Look at the current roster by position and graduation year, then ask the coach directly whether they are recruiting that position in your athlete's class.

**What matters most when choosing schools for the list?**
Level, major, cost after aid, distance, and whether they run camps your athlete can attend. Cut anything missing the major.

**How often should the target list be updated?**
Every few months, and whenever film improves or a level stops responding. A list that never changes is not being worked.
