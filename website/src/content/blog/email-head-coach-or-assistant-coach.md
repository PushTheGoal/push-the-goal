Your athlete has the target list, the film and a first email ready to go. Then they open a school's staff page and find a head coach, two assistants, a goalkeeper coach and somebody called the recruiting coordinator. Who gets the email?

Short answer: the head coach and the assistant who runs recruiting. Every time.

## Why the assistant matters as much as the head coach

At a lot of programs, the head coach is not the first person reading recruiting email. An assistant runs the recruiting calendar, sorts the inbox, watches the film first and builds the list of players the staff goes to see. That assistant decides whether your athlete's name ever reaches the head coach.

Email only the head coach and your athlete may be writing to the busiest inbox in the building. Email only an assistant and the person who makes the final call may not see the name for months.

So write to both.

## How to find the right people

Start with the program's staff page on the athletics website. Look for:

- **The head coach.** Always on the list.
- **An assistant with "recruiting coordinator" in the title.** If one is listed, that is the most important email on the page.
- **The position coach.** Keepers should also write to the goalkeeper coach. My post on [goalkeeper recruiting](/goalkeeper-recruiting/) covers why that coach matters so much for keepers.

If nobody is labeled recruiting coordinator, email the head coach and the first assistant.

## Send one email or several?

Write one email and address it to the coaches by name in the greeting: "Coach Smith and Coach Jones." Send it to both addresses. Do not paste the same email to each coach separately with only the name swapped, because coaches compare notes and form emails stand out for the wrong reason.

The email itself follows the same structure as any first contact: grad year and position in the subject line, one personal line about the program, the key details, a film link, the upcoming schedule and a closing question. The full format is in my [guide to emailing college coaches](/how-to-email-college-soccer-coaches/).

## Who gets the follow-up

Whoever answers. The moment a coach replies, that coach owns the conversation. Reply to them, keep the rest of the staff copied if they were on the first email, and stay in whatever channel they used. My rule with my athletes: if a coach texts, keep it on text. Do not switch them back to email because it feels more formal.

If nobody answers, the follow-ups go to the same people as the first email. Something new each time: fresh film, results, the next tournament schedule. And when a coach who was writing back goes quiet, read [how to restart the conversation](/when-college-coaches-stop-responding/) before sending another "just checking in."

## Coaches move. The relationship goes with them.

Assistants get promoted, take head coaching jobs and change schools all the time. That is one more reason to build a relationship with the assistant and not only the head coach. I tell every athlete the same thing: "We always take the calls with whatever level, because they can switch." The assistant your athlete writes to this year may be the head coach at a different program in two years, and they will remember who wrote to them.

## Mistakes to skip

**Writing to the general athletics inbox.** It goes nowhere. Find the coaches' own addresses.

**Copying every coach in the department.** Write to the soccer staff for your athlete's team and nobody else.

**Parents sending it.** Coaches want to hear from the athlete. Parents help behind the scenes with the list, the proofreading and the calendar.

**Using an old staff list.** Staffs change every year. Check the staff page before every round of emails.

## FAQ

**Should my athlete email the head coach or the assistant coach?**
Both. Address one email to the head coach and the assistant who runs recruiting, usually listed as the recruiting coordinator on the staff page.

**What is a recruiting coordinator in college soccer?**
An assistant coach who manages the program's recruiting: the inbox, the film, the event calendar and the list of players the staff goes to watch.

**Should a goalkeeper email the goalkeeper coach?**
Yes. Add the goalkeeper coach to the first email alongside the head coach and the recruiting coordinator.

**What if a different coach replies than the one my athlete wrote to?**
Reply to whoever answered. That coach is now the contact, and the conversation stays with them.
