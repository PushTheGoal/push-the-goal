Every family I talk to has either paid for a recruiting platform or is about to.

The pitch is reasonable: a profile, a database of college programs, some tools, a person who checks in. What families hear underneath it is different, and it is the source of the disappointment. They hear *we will get your kid recruited.*

That is worth being precise about, because the two things cost similar money and produce different outcomes.

## What a platform gives you

**A hosted profile.** A page with your athlete's details and film. Useful. Also something a YouTube channel and a free page can do, as we cover in [building a recruiting profile](/soccer-recruiting-profile/).

**A school database.** Searchable listings with coach contact details. Genuinely convenient, and the same information is publicly available on athletic department websites.

**Bulk messaging.** The ability to send to many programs at once. This is the part that sounds like the most value and delivers the least, for a reason worth understanding.

**Some coaching or check-ins.** Varies enormously by package and by who you get.

## What a coach sees on the other end

Coaches can tell a mass message from a real one in a single line.

A note that could have been sent to four hundred programs reads as exactly that. It gets deleted the way you delete a marketing email, and no amount of volume fixes it, because the problem is the message rather than the number of them.

The emails that get replies name something true about that specific program. One honest sentence. That takes your athlete ninety seconds per school and it cannot be automated, which is precisely why it works.

## What a strategy is

A strategy is not a product. It is a sequence, and it is the same one every recruited athlete runs.

**An honest level assessment**, so the list is real. **A target list built wide**, fifty to a hundred schools across divisions. **Film that earns a live look.** **Personal outreach from the athlete**, every school, in their own words. **Follow-up every week or two** with something new each time. **Event choices** that follow the list rather than the other way round. **Conversations converted** into visits and decisions.

Nobody sells you that in a subscription, because most of it is work only your family can do.

## The honest comparison

A platform is a tool. A strategy is the thing that uses it.

Families who fail bought the tool and assumed it was the strategy. Their athlete has a beautiful profile nobody visits, because a profile is a destination and nothing is driving traffic to it. Coaches do not browse databases hunting for players. They read emails and watch film sent by athletes who wrote to them.

Families who succeed may or may not have a platform. What they have in common is the sequence above, run consistently for eighteen months.

## Can you do this yourselves?

On paper, yes. In practice, the families who pull it off alone are rare, and I want to be honest with you about why.

It is not knowledge. Everything in the sequence above is public, and you could run it from this blog post. It is that the plan needs someone driving it for eighteen months while everything else in your life keeps happening.

Here is how it goes. September is great. Your athlete sends thirty emails and the target list looks real. Then club season starts, midterms hit, you are driving to three tournaments a month, and somebody gets sick. The follow-ups slip a week, then three. By February nobody has emailed a coach since November, and the schools that would have said yes have filled their class.

Nobody planned that. Parents are busy and athletes are busier, and recruiting is the thing with no deadline attached until suddenly it has passed. That is the failure mode I see over and over, and it has nothing to do with how much the family cared.

So what outside guidance buys you is not access to information. It is judgment and accountability. An honest read on level, so you stop spending months on schools that were never going to happen. Someone who has read hundreds of these emails telling you why yours is not landing. And somebody asking, every single week, whether the follow-ups went out.

Consistency is what produces the result. Almost nobody is consistent on their own.

## FAQ

**Are soccer recruiting services worth it?**
It depends what you are buying. A hosted profile and a school database are conveniences you can replicate free. Honest level assessment, outreach coaching and accountability are the parts that change outcomes.

**Do college coaches use recruiting platforms to find players?**
Rarely as a discovery tool. Coaches build lists from athletes who contact them directly, plus film and event evaluations.

**Can we do college soccer recruiting ourselves?**
The information is all public, so in principle yes. Few families manage it, and the reason is consistency rather than knowledge. Recruiting has no deadline attached until the deadline has passed, and it is the first thing to slip when a season gets busy.

**Why do mass emails not work?**
Coaches recognize them instantly. One true sentence about that specific program is what separates a read email from a deleted one.

**What is the one thing families most often skip?**
Follow-up. One email is an introduction. The recruitment happens in the fifth, sixth and seventh contact.
