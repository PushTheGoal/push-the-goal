Your athlete commits. The Instagram post goes up, the family group chat celebrates, and everybody relaxes.

Then in April the coach takes a job three states away, and the new staff has never heard of your athlete.

That scenario is not rare, and families who understand the difference between a verbal and a signature handle it far better than families who thought the recruiting was over.

## What a verbal commitment is

A promise. Your athlete says they will attend, the coach says they want them, and both sides mean it at the time.

It carries no legal weight. Either side can walk away with no penalty. That is the design, and both sides know it.

Verbals do carry real weight in practice. Coaches who break them get reputations, and so do athletes. Most verbals hold. The ones that break, break for reasons nobody planned.

**What breaks a verbal from the program's side:** the coach leaves, the staff gets fired, a better recruit becomes available late, or the athlete's play or grades drop off.

**What breaks it from the athlete's side:** a better offer, a coaching change, a campus visit somewhere else that changes their mind, or growing up and wanting something different at seventeen than they wanted at fifteen.

## What signing is

A written agreement between your athlete and the institution. The school commits to athletic aid for a period, and your athlete commits to attend.

The important detail families miss: the letter binds your athlete to the **school**, not to the coach. If the coach leaves the week after signing, your athlete is still committed to the institution. Releases exist and can be requested, but they are not automatic.

The letter of intent system has changed recently, and how it works varies by level. Division III does not offer athletic scholarships, so there is no athletic letter, and those commitments run through admissions and financial aid instead. NAIA and JUCO have their own paperwork.

Ask the coach what your athlete will sign, and when. Get the answer specific to their level and class.

## The gap in between

Izi's rule here is simple. If your athlete has a verbal from a program they want, sign the first day there is something to sign. A verbal locks nobody in, so every week between the promise and the signature is a week where something can change.

Most of the risk lives here, in the months between a verbal and a signature. It can be years for an athlete who commits early.

**Keep recruiting quietly.** Not aggressively, and not in a way that damages the relationship with the program your athlete committed to. Stay responsive to other coaches. Keep the film current. Izi's line about this is that any coach relationship is worth keeping, because coaches move.

An athlete who goes completely dark for eighteen months and then loses their spot in March has to start from zero with no relationships left.

**Stay in contact with the program.** Send updates. Send new film. Attend a game. An athlete who disappears after committing gives a staff no reason to defend their spot when the class gets tight.

**Watch the coaching situation.** If a head coach is rumored for another job, ask directly. Coaches will not always answer, and how they handle the question tells you something.

**Keep the grades where they need to be.** Commitments get pulled over transcripts more often than over soccer.

## When a verbal breaks

If a program pulls out, your athlete has been let down and it is worth being angry about for a day. Then move.

Go back to the target list. Email the programs that were interested before, tell them plainly that circumstances changed and your athlete is available. Coaches understand this completely, because they watch it happen every year. Some will move fast, because a recruit they liked is suddenly free.

This is the strongest argument for keeping the list warm. The families who recover in weeks are the ones with relationships still open. The families who recover in months are starting over.

If your athlete wants to break a verbal, they should call the coach. Not email, not have a parent do it, not go silent. It is an uncomfortable five minutes that protects a reputation in a small world where coaches talk.

## The frame to keep

A verbal is a good sign and it is not the finish line. It means a program wants your athlete today.

Celebrate it. Then keep the film current, keep the grades up, keep the relationships alive, and sign the first day there is something to sign. Recruiting is over when your athlete is on campus.

## FAQ

**Is a verbal commitment binding in college soccer?**
No. Neither side is bound, and either can walk away without penalty. Most verbals hold, but they are promises rather than contracts. If your athlete is sure about the school, sign at the first opportunity.

**Can a college pull a soccer scholarship after a verbal commitment?**
Yes. It most often happens when a coaching staff changes, when a late recruit becomes available, or when an athlete's grades or play fall off.

**What happens if the coach leaves after my athlete signs?**
The agreement is with the school, not the coach. Your athlete remains committed to the institution, though a release can be requested.

**Should my athlete keep talking to other coaches after committing?**
Quietly, yes. Keep relationships open and film current. An athlete who disappears for a year has nothing to fall back on if the commitment breaks.

**How does my athlete back out of a verbal commitment?**
With a phone call to the coach, made by the athlete. Direct and early protects a reputation in a small profession where coaches talk.
