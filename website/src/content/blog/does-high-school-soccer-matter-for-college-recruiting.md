Somewhere around freshman year, every club family hears it: "College coaches don't watch high school soccer." Some families take that to mean the high school season is a waste of time. Some clubs encourage it.

Most college coaches do the bulk of their evaluating at club showcases, because that is where they can see many recruits in one weekend. But the high school season still does real work for your athlete's recruiting, and families who skip it give up more than they think.

## What the high school season gives your athlete

**Minutes.** A player who starts every high school game gets dozens of competitive games a year. Minutes build the decisions, fitness and confidence that show up at the next showcase. Minutes matter more than the badge on the jersey.

**Film.** High school games are filmed more often than families realize, and good clips are good clips wherever they come from. A goal, a save or a defensive stop from a varsity game belongs in the reel. My guide to [making a recruiting video](/how-to-make-a-soccer-recruiting-video/) covers how to pull them.

**Leadership.** Captaining a high school team, organizing a back line or leading younger players tells a coach something a club stat line cannot. College coaches recruit people they want in their locker room.

**A second coach who can vouch.** A high school coach who has watched your athlete for four seasons is a reference. Make sure that coach knows which colleges your athlete is talking to.

## Where high school soccer matters more

**Some levels and regions.** Coaches at plenty of D3, NAIA and junior college programs recruit close to home and do come to high school games, especially playoff games. If those programs are on your athlete's list, the high school season is a real stage.

**Smaller communities.** In towns without a big club nearby, the high school team may be the strongest competition around. I cover how to build a plan around that in my post on [getting recruited from a small town](/small-town-soccer-recruiting/).

**Late developers.** An athlete who grew or improved late often has their best film from the high school season. Use it.

## When club and high school conflict

Some club programs ask players to skip the high school season. Others leave it to the family. If your athlete is facing that choice, ask three questions:

1. **Where will they play more meaningful minutes?** Minutes against good opposition beat time on a bench, wherever the bench is.
2. **Where are the coaches on the target list watching?** If the list is full of programs that recruit at club showcases, the club schedule comes first. If it includes local programs that watch high school games, the high school season counts.
3. **What does the athlete want?** Playing with friends for their school is part of the game. An athlete who loves the season plays better in it.

Your athlete's [club coach](/what-your-club-coach-will-and-wont-do/) can help with this decision, but it is your family's decision to make.

## Take the open spot

Sometimes the high school team needs your athlete somewhere new. I worked with a keeper who was headed for a season of split halves on JV, because another keeper was coming in. Then a spot opened at outside back on varsity. My answer: "You see the opening, take it." The athlete earns varsity minutes, and as I told her, playing in front of the goal teaches a keeper more about how the defense is supposed to work.

## Put the high school season to work

- Add varsity starts, captaincy and honors to the [recruiting profile](/soccer-recruiting-profile/).
- Pull film from every high school game you can get footage of.
- Send coaches the high school schedule when it includes games they could attend, playoffs especially.
- Keep the high school coach in the loop as a reference.

## FAQ

**Do college coaches watch high school soccer games?**
Some do, especially at D3, NAIA and junior college programs that recruit close to home, and at playoff games. Most college evaluation still happens at club showcases.

**Should my athlete skip high school soccer to focus on club?**
Only if the club requires it or the high school season would cost meaningful minutes at a higher level. For many athletes, the high school season adds minutes, film and leadership that help their recruiting.

**Does playing varsity matter for college recruiting?**
Varsity minutes and honors belong on the recruiting profile. They show coaches an athlete who competes and leads, even if most evaluation happens at club events.

**Can high school game film go in a recruiting video?**
Yes. Good clips are good clips. Use the strongest plays, wherever they came from.
