"Are we too late?"

If you are asking that question, I already know two things about your family. First, your athlete loves this game and wants to keep playing after high school. Second, somewhere along the way, someone made you feel like the recruiting window closed while you were not looking.

So let me give you the honest answer, the one I give families on real calls every week.

## The short answer

No, it is probably not too late. But the window is not open forever, and from this point forward, how you spend the next 60 days matters more than the last three years.

Here is what most families do not realize: the dramatic early-commitment stories you see on social media are the exception, not the rule. Across Division I, Division II, Division III, NAIA, and junior college soccer, thousands of roster spots get filled during junior year, senior year, and even the spring before college starts. Coaches lose committed players to transfers, injuries, grades, and changed minds every single season. Rosters have holes in them right now.

The families who find those spots are not the most talented. They are the ones who move with a plan.

## Why it feels too late (and why that feeling lies)

The recruiting anxiety most parents carry comes from comparison. Another kid on the club team committed sophomore year. Someone in a Facebook group said D1 rosters are done by junior year. A club coach shrugged and said "we'll see."

Comparison is a terrible recruiting strategy. The player who committed early is running her race. Your athlete needs to run theirs, and the truth about their race is this: a junior or senior with good film, honest grades, and a focused outreach plan is a real recruit today. A talented player with none of those things is invisible, whether they are a freshman or a senior.

Too late is not a grade level. Too late is deciding to keep waiting.

## The late-recruiting plan: what to do this month

If your athlete is a junior or senior and starting from zero, here is the plan. Not someday. This month.

**1. Get film together within two weeks.** Coaches will not guess at what your athlete can do. Pull the best moments from recent games into a three to four minute highlight video, best clips first. Done beats perfect. A senior with decent film in September beats a senior with perfect film in February.

**2. Build a realistic school list, 50 to 70 programs.** Think bigger than feels reasonable. Most families come to me with a list of twelve, and a list that size cannot absorb the nos that late recruiting produces. The list also has to match reality on three fronts: athletic level, academics, and money. Include D3, NAIA, and junior college programs on purpose, not as a consolation prize. Those levels recruit later, offer real academic and athletic money, and hold rosters full of players who could have played higher and chose the right fit instead.

**3. Email every coach on the list, personally.** A short, specific email: graduation year, position, club, GPA, film link, upcoming schedule. Coaches can tell a template blast from a real note in one line. We break down the exact format in our guide to [emailing college soccer coaches](/how-to-email-college-soccer-coaches/).

**4. Follow up like it is a job search, because it is.** One email is a raindrop. Your athlete should update coaches every one to two weeks with new film, results, and schedule changes. Persistence reads as seriousness, and coaches recruit serious.

**5. Be honest about the level.** This is the hardest one, and it is where a knowledgeable outside voice earns its keep. Chasing only D1 in February of senior year is how athletes end up not playing at all. The goal is not a division. The goal is four more years of soccer at a school that fits.

## What late recruiting cannot survive

I promised honesty, so here it is. The late path is real, but it cannot survive three things: waiting another season to start, grades that shut doors coaches cannot reopen, and pride that says no to every level except one. If your family can avoid those three traps, there is almost always a path.

I have watched athletes go from zero coach contact to committed in a matter of months. Not because of luck. Because they stopped wondering if it was too late and started working a real plan.

## FAQ

**Can a senior still get recruited for college soccer?**
Yes. Coaches fill roster spots through senior year and into the spring, especially at D3, NAIA, and junior college programs. Seniors need to move quickly: film ready, a realistic list, and direct outreach to coaches within weeks, not months.

**Do college coaches recruit juniors?**
Heavily. Junior year is the busiest recruiting window in college soccer. A junior starting today is late to the ideal timeline but right on time for a huge share of real recruiting activity.
