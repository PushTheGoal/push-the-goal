The moment a coach gets interested, they type your athlete's name into a search bar.

That happens before a call, before a visit, and often before a reply. Whatever comes back either supports the impression your athlete has built or quietly undoes it. Most families never think about this step, and it is happening on every recruit.

## What a coach is checking

**Is this athlete easy to find?** A locked account with no name and no grad year is a dead end. A coach with ninety other recruits does not investigate.

**Does the film match the email?** They are looking for confirmation that the player in the highlight reel is the player they were told about.

**What is this person like?** Tone, how they talk about teammates and opponents, how they handle a loss.

**Anything disqualifying?** This is the short version: nothing that would embarrass a program. Coaches are risk-averse, and when choosing between similar players they will use anything to break the tie.

## The account that helps

Your athlete does not need to be an influencer. They need an account that answers a coach's questions in ten seconds.

**Bio with the essentials.** Name, graduation year, position, club, and the film link. That is the whole job of the bio.

**A public account, or a public second one.** If your athlete wants their personal account private, fine. Make a soccer account that is public and put that in every email.

**Film pinned and current.** The highlight reel and recent clips near the top.

**Post the training, not only the goals.** Technical work, fitness, rehab, small-sided reps. Coaches are evaluating habits, and training content says more about a player's standards than a finish does.

**Caption so a stranger understands.** Position, what the clip shows, grad year. A coach scrolling should never have to guess.

## The thing that grows a soccer account

One of my athletes went from a handful of clips to reels in the hundreds of thousands of views by doing two unglamorous things: marking herself in the opening seconds so viewers knew who to watch, and captioning technical content so people understood what they were seeing.

That is the whole trick. Great content is mostly about getting it out there consistently. Getting your name out there is the biggest thing, and your athlete is already on their phone. They might as well be posting.

Three times a week is enough. Consistency beats production value, every time.

## Where this connects to recruiting

Social media does not get anyone recruited on its own. No coach offers a scholarship because of a reel.

What it does is remove friction. A coach who wants to check your athlete finds a clean account with film and grad year, and the conversation keeps moving. It also lets coaches see development over months rather than one polished highlight package, which is genuinely useful to them.

And it works alongside the parts that do the heavy lifting: the film, the [recruiting profile](/soccer-recruiting-profile/), and the emails. Social media is the confirmation, not the pitch.

## A short checklist for this week

Set the bio. Make the account public or build a soccer one. Pin the film. Post three times a week, including training. Look at the last thirty posts the way a stranger would and clean up what needs it. Put the film link everywhere the same way, so every coach lands in one place.

That is a Saturday afternoon of work that pays off every time a coach types your athlete's name.

## FAQ

**Do college coaches look at recruits' social media?**
Yes, routinely, usually as soon as they become interested and before a call or visit.

**Should my athlete's account be public?**
The soccer account should be. Keep a personal account private if you prefer, and put the public soccer one in every email.

**What should a soccer recruiting Instagram bio say?**
Name, graduation year, position, club and a link to the highlight film. Nothing else is required.

**Can social media get my athlete recruited?**
Not on its own. It removes friction and shows development, while film, outreach and follow-up do the actual work.

**How often should my athlete post?**
About three times a week, including training and technical clips rather than only goals. Consistency matters more than production quality.
