Parents who went through this ten years ago keep telling you it worked differently for them. They are right, and the reason is worth understanding, because it changes what your athlete should be doing.

## The mechanic

College players can enter a portal and move to another school with far less friction than before. Thousands of soccer players do it every year.

Put yourself in a coach's chair. A starting center back graduates. Two options:

Sign a high school senior. Bring them in, hope the projection is right, wait a year or two for them to be ready.

Or take a junior from another program who has started thirty college games, whose film is against college opposition, and who can play in August.

The second option is faster and safer. Coaches take it constantly.

## What that does to your athlete

**Fewer freshman spots at the top.** The most competitive programs increasingly fill needs with transfers. High school recruits still get in, and the door is narrower than it was.

**Recruiting runs later.** Coaches hold spots to see what the portal offers in the spring. A program that would have committed to your athlete in November now waits until March. Families read the silence as rejection when it is a coach keeping options open.

**Freshmen play less at the highest levels.** A roster with several experienced transfers has less room for a first-year to develop in games.

**Development levels matter more.** Programs a level or two down from the biggest names are where your athlete plays real minutes, gets real film, and becomes exactly the player the top programs want two years later.

## What to do about it

**Widen the list, and go down as well as up.** This is the practical answer. If the top of the market is buying experience, the way in is to go somewhere your athlete plays, then let the film do the arguing. Fifty to a hundred schools, across levels.

**Take the two-year route seriously.** [Junior college](/juco-soccer-recruiting/) went from a fallback to a strategy. Two years of college minutes turns your athlete into the proven player coaches now prefer. Izi took that route to Division I before the portal existed, and the logic is stronger now than it was then.

**Read late silence correctly.** A program that has not said no has not said no. Keep following up into the spring. Coaches who go quiet in December are sometimes back in April with a need they did not have before.

**Ask the question directly.** "Are you recruiting my position in my class, or are you planning to fill it through the portal?" Coaches answer this. It saves your family months of guessing, and it is the kind of direct question that makes coaches take an athlete seriously.

**Do not commit early out of fear.** Scarcity makes families grab the first offer. An athlete who commits at fifteen to a program that is wrong for them ends up in the portal themselves.

## Why the clock starts earlier now

Every change above pushes the same way. The door at the top is narrower and the decisions come later, so the athletes who get through are the ones who started the work early and kept at it longest.

That work starts before high school. Grades 5 through 8 are where an athlete builds the habits, the grades, the film routine and the confidence to talk to adults that this market now rewards. It is why Izi built [Junior Prep](/junior-prep-coaching/). A family that starts in ninth grade is on time. A family that starts in eleventh is competing with college juniors for the same roster spot.

## The part worth saying out loud

This is harder than it was. Pretending otherwise does not help anyone.

It is also not closed. Programs still need freshmen, because a roster built entirely from transfers has no continuity and no culture, and coaches know it. Every program recruits high school players every year.

What the portal punished is the passive approach. Families who send twelve emails to twelve dream schools and wait have a worse experience than they would have had a decade ago. Families who build a wide list, work it across levels, follow up through the spring, and stay open to the two-year route are doing fine.

Controlling the controllables is the whole job here. Your athlete cannot change how coaches fill rosters. They can control their film, their grades, their outreach, and how long they keep going after most families have stopped.

## FAQ

**Has the transfer portal made high school soccer recruiting harder?**
At the most competitive programs, yes. Coaches can fill a need with a proven college player instead of projecting a freshman. Programs still recruit high school players every year.

**Why are college coaches responding later than they used to?**
Many hold roster spots into the spring to see what becomes available through the portal. Silence in December is often a coach keeping options open rather than a no.

**Should my athlete aim lower because of the portal?**
Aim wider. Include levels where your athlete would play real minutes, because college film is what makes an athlete attractive to programs above them later.

**Does the transfer portal make junior college a better option?**
It strengthens the case. Two years of college minutes turn your athlete into the experienced player that coaches now prefer to sign.

**How do I find out if a program will use the portal instead of recruiting my athlete?**
Ask directly whether they are recruiting that position in your athlete's class or planning to fill it through the portal. Coaches answer this question.
