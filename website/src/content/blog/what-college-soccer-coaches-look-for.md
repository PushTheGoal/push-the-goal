A coach came to watch one of my athletes and spent most of the game looking at her when she did not have the ball.

That is the part families never see coming. Your highlight reel gets a coach to the field. What happens in the eighty-seven minutes your athlete is not on the video is what decides whether they want her.

Here is what they are watching.

## Between the plays

**Reaction to a mistake.** Every player gives the ball away. Coaches watch the next ten seconds. Does your athlete sprint to win it back, or drop their head and complain? This one is enormous, and it is visible within minutes.

Make this its own piece of film. A short reel or a playlist labeled recovery runs, with clips that clearly start at the turnover so a coach sees the mistake and then sees the response. Most athletes bury this inside a highlight reel where it reads as ordinary defending. Labeled and separated, it becomes the clearest evidence of character you can hand a coach, and almost nobody sends one.

**Work without the ball.** Defensive shape, recovery runs, checking shoulders, communicating. A player who only exists when the ball arrives is a passenger, and coaches see it immediately.

**Body language when it is going badly.** Down two goals, bad call, teammate making mistakes. A coach is imagining your athlete in their locker room in November when the season is not going to plan.

## On the bench

Coaches watch substitutes. How your athlete behaves when they come off, whether they are engaged in the game or on their phone, whether they encourage the player who replaced them.

Four years is a long time to coach somebody who sulks.

## With the referee and their teammates

Arguing with officials is a fast way to lose a coach's interest. So is snapping at teammates. Coaches recruit people they will spend more hours with than most of their own family, and they screen hard for who is easy to be around.

The flip side is worth knowing: an athlete who lifts teammates gets noticed for it, even in a losing performance.

## With you

Coaches watch parents. They watch how you behave on the sideline, whether you coach from the touchline, whether you are the reason your kid looks up after every mistake.

I say this gently because it comes from care: your conduct is part of your athlete's recruitment. A coach who likes a player and does not like the family situation sometimes moves on without ever explaining why.

## In the inbox

Almost every trait above shows up in how your athlete handles their own outreach.

Do they email themselves or does a parent? Do they follow up, or send once and quit? Do they answer a coach's question directly? Do they reply within a day? Coaches read consistency as maturity and maturity as coachability, and they are forming that judgment long before they watch a game. My [guide to emailing college coaches](/how-to-email-college-soccer-coaches/) covers the mechanics.

## What this means for your athlete this week

The good news is that all of this is controllable, and none of it requires more talent.

Sprint after a turnover. Talk on the field. Be the first one up when a teammate goes down. Stay in the game from the bench. Send your own emails and answer them fast. That is a list any player can execute starting Saturday, and it moves your athlete ahead of players with better highlight reels.

Talent gets you evaluated. This is what gets you picked.

## FAQ

**What do college soccer coaches look for in a player?**
Speed of decision and technical ability first, then everything around it: reaction to mistakes, work without the ball, body language, treatment of teammates and officials, and how the athlete handles their own communication.

**Do college coaches watch players when they do not have the ball?**
Yes, and it is often most of what they watch. Off-ball work and reactions reveal more than highlights do.

**Does my behavior as a parent affect recruiting?**
It can. Coaches notice sideline conduct and factor in the family they would be dealing with for four years.

**Is talent enough to get recruited?**
No. Talent gets an athlete evaluated. Attitude, work rate and communication decide between players of similar ability, and those are the things any athlete can improve this week.

**How do coaches judge attitude from film?**
They mostly do not, which is why the live look matters and why what happens between plays counts so much once they are watching.
