A college coach opens forty recruiting emails in a morning. Thirty-eight are the same email. Grad year, position, club, highlight link, thank you for your time.

Your athlete's is one of the thirty-eight, and it is a good one, and it is still one of the thirty-eight.

The thing that breaks that pattern is ninety seconds of your athlete talking to a camera.

## What it is

A short video, filmed on a phone, where your athlete introduces themselves. Who they are, where they come from, what soccer has given them, what they want from a program.

Not a highlight reel. The reel answers how they play. This answers who is going to be in the locker room for four years, which is the question a coach is asking and cannot get from film.

Izi pushes every athlete she works with to make one, because generic form emails do not stand out and this does. One of her athletes got a substantive reply from a major Division I program off the back of hers, after the standard emails had produced nothing.

## Why it works on coaches

A coach is recruiting a person. They are going to travel with this athlete, coach them through a bad run of form, and answer to a locker room that has to hold together.

Film cannot tell them whether an athlete is coachable, whether they can talk, whether they will handle being a freshman. Ninety seconds of your athlete speaking tells them a great deal about all three.

It also proves effort. Anyone can attach a link. Making a personal video for a specific program means your athlete cared enough to do the thing almost nobody does. Coaches read that correctly.

## The structure

Izi's outline, in the order she teaches it:

**Open with the basics.** Name, graduating year, position, high school and club, state. Ten seconds. A coach needs the file details immediately.

**The origin story.** How your athlete came to the sport, and why they stayed. Short and real. The kid who started because an older sibling played, or who was hopeless in the first season and refused to quit. Specific beats polished.

**What the position taught them.** The heart of it. A center back talks about organizing people. A keeper talks about carrying a mistake and moving on. A midfielder talks about seeing the whole field. This is where a coach hears maturity, and it is the part that separates a good video from a nice one.

**Academics.** GPA, honors or AP classes, what they want to study. Brief and factual.

**Life outside soccer.** Family, a job, a second sport, something they care about. Coaches want a whole person.

**What they want in a program.** What they are looking for in a coach, a team, a campus. If the video is going to one school, name it and say why. That specificity is the point.

Ninety seconds to two minutes. Longer stops getting watched.

## Making it

A phone, propped up, decent light, quiet room. Nobody wants production value. They want to see your athlete.

Look at the lens, not the screen. Notes off camera are fine. Do not read a script word for word, because a coach can hear it instantly and it defeats the whole purpose.

Do several takes and keep the one that sounds like your athlete on a good day. Slightly nervous and genuine beats smooth and hollow.

Upload it unlisted on YouTube, next to the highlight reel, and link both from the same place. Keep everything your athlete has in one spot.

## Sending it

**Include it in the email, do not replace the reel with it.** Both links, clearly labeled.

**Personalize the opening line** to the school and the coach. A video that mentions the program by name is hard to ignore.

**Time it well.** Izi's tip is to send at the end of July, when coach inboxes quiet down and your athlete's message has room to land. Any quiet period works. Avoid the weeks when every athlete in the country is emailing.

**Send it to the coaches who went silent.** A dead thread is exactly where this belongs. It is new, it is not another "circling back", and it gives a coach a reason to open.

## The reason nobody does it

Recording yourself talking about yourself is uncomfortable at sixteen. Athletes stall for weeks.

That discomfort is the whole reason it works. If it were easy, every athlete would send one and it would be another thirty-eight-out-of-forty email.

Get your athlete to record a bad first version this week. It will be bad. The second one will be fine, and fine is all it needs to be.

## FAQ

**What is a personal statement video in soccer recruiting?**
A short phone video where your athlete introduces themselves to a coach: background, what their position taught them, academics, life outside soccer, and what they want in a program.

**How long should it be?**
Ninety seconds to two minutes. Coaches stop watching longer ones.

**Does it replace the highlight video?**
No. The reel shows how your athlete plays and the personal video shows who they are. Send both.

**When is the best time to send one?**
During a quiet period in coach inboxes. Izi points athletes at the end of July, when volume drops and a personal message stands out.

**Does my athlete need good equipment?**
No. A phone, decent light and a quiet room. Coaches want to see the athlete, not the production.
