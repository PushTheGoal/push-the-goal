When a coach gets interested in your athlete, the first thing they do is look them up.

What they find in the next sixty seconds decides whether the interest survives. A clean profile with film, grades and contact details keeps the conversation going. A dead link, a locked account, or nothing at all ends it quietly, and you never learn it happened.

Your athlete's profile is the landing page for every email they send. Build it once, properly.

## What belongs on it

**Name, graduation year, position, club and high school.** Grad year first. It is the field a coach filters by, and an athlete whose year is unclear gets skipped.

**Height, dominant foot, jersey number.** Small details that let a coach find your kid on a field of twenty-two.

**GPA, and test scores if they help.** Academics widen the list of schools that can take your athlete. Leaving grades off makes coaches assume the worst.

**The film link, above everything else.** The highlight video is the whole reason the profile exists. Put it at the top, make it work without a login, and check the link from a phone. My [guide to making a recruiting video](/how-to-make-a-soccer-recruiting-video/) covers what goes in it.

**Upcoming schedule.** Events, dates, and which team. This is what converts a coach's existing travel into a look at your athlete.

**Contact details for the athlete.** Their email, their phone. Not yours. Coaches are recruiting a young adult.

**One short paragraph in your athlete's own voice.** What they want to study, what kind of program fits them, what they are working on. Three or four sentences. It is the only part that sounds like a person.

## Build it as a brand, not a form

Here is where I part ways with a lot of recruiting advice. Most of it tells you to strip the profile down to a bare file card. I push the opposite with my athletes.

There is one rule worth keeping from the minimalist camp: nothing goes between the coach and the film. Put the video at the top and make it open in one tap. A coach who has to hunt for the video stops hunting.

Everything after that should sound and look like a specific person.

**A real paragraph in their own words.** Not a résumé summary. What they want to study, what kind of program fits them, what they are working on this season.

**A personal statement video.** Ninety seconds of your athlete talking to a coach. Almost nobody sends one, which is why it lands.

**A consistent look.** Same photo, same colors, same handle across the profile, the film and the socials. A coach who sees your athlete three times should recognize them the third time.

**Links to where they post.** Training clips, technical work, the season as it happens. Coaches want to see development, and that only exists if your athlete is publishing it.

Getting your name out there is the biggest thing in this whole process. A profile that reads like a database entry does none of that work. A profile that reads like a player does.

## Where to host it

Keep everything in one spot so any coach who gets curious lands in the same place.

A YouTube channel for the film is the simplest foundation. It works on every device, needs no account, and the link never expires. Put that link in the Instagram bio, in every email signature, and on whatever page holds the rest of the details.

Recruiting platforms will host a profile for you, and some families find them convenient. Understand what you are buying: a page. It does not do the outreach, and coaches do not browse those databases hunting for players. If the platform replaces your athlete's emails, it is costing you recruiting.

A free page you control, linked from a YouTube channel, does the same job.

## The part families forget

Coaches look your athlete up everywhere, not only where you point them.

That means the public Instagram, the tagged photos, the comment sections. A coach deciding between two similar players will use anything to break the tie, and an ugly account breaks it the wrong way. This is not about being boring. It is about not handing anyone an easy reason to move on.

Have your athlete look at their own accounts the way a stranger would, then fix what needs fixing before the outreach starts.

## Keep it current

A profile built in September and never touched is worth less by November. New film, new results, an updated schedule, a better GPA. Every refresh is also a reason to send a follow-up email, which is the whole engine of recruiting anyway.

Your athlete owns this. They build it, they update it, they send it. An athlete who runs their own profile reads as coachable before a coach has watched a single clip.

## FAQ

**What should be on a soccer recruiting profile?**
Grad year, position, club and high school, height and dominant foot, GPA, a working film link at the top, upcoming schedule, and the athlete's own contact details.

**Do I need to pay for a recruiting profile service?**
No. Those services host a page, they do not do the outreach, and coaches do not browse them looking for players. A YouTube channel plus a simple page you control does the same job.

**Where should we host the highlight video?**
YouTube. It opens on any device without a login, and the link stays stable across every email and profile.

**Do college coaches look at social media?**
Yes. When a coach gets interested they look your athlete up, so public accounts should be presentable before outreach begins.

**How often should the profile be updated?**
Every time there is new film, a new result, an updated schedule or a better GPA. Each update is also a reason to follow up with coaches.
