This question comes up on nearly every call I take, usually with a number attached. The family has been told that the next tier of club soccer costs several thousand more a year, and that without it their kid will not get seen.

So here is the straight answer: no, you do not need it. And it helps.

Both of those are true, and the gap between them is where families waste money.

## What those leagues buy you

**Concentrated evaluation.** The real product is not the coaching or the jersey. It is that college coaches show up to those events in volume, because many recruits play in one place. That is a genuine advantage and worth paying for if you use it.

**A known competitive level.** When a coach sees that league on your athlete's profile, they have a rough sense of the standard your kid plays against. It saves them a step.

**Better teammates.** Training against stronger players develops your athlete. That is worth something regardless of recruiting.

## What they do not buy you

**They do not get your athlete recruited.** This is the part families pay for and do not receive. Playing in a top league puts your athlete in the building. It does not put their name on a coach's list. Coaches arrive at those events with a schedule built weeks earlier from athletes who emailed them.

I have watched families spend a fortune on the league and nothing on the process, then wonder why the fields were full of coaches and none of them were watching their kid. The coaches were there for the players who wrote to them.

**They do not replace film.** Coaches still decide from video before they decide to watch live.

**They do not guarantee minutes.** A player sitting on a top-league bench is less recruitable than a player starting and leading a level down. Coaches want to see your athlete play, and they cannot evaluate someone who is not on the field.

## The question to ask instead

Not "are we in the right league" but "are we getting evaluated."

An athlete in a mid-level club who emails fifty coaches, sends film, tells them which field and what time, and follows up every week or two will out-recruit a top-league athlete who does none of that. Every time. The system beats the badge.

If the money is a stretch, the honest ranking of where it goes furthest is this: film first, then targeted events where your specific list of schools attends, then the league. Most families do it in exactly the reverse order.

## When it is worth it

**Your athlete is starting and playing real minutes there.** If the move means a bench role, wait a year.

**You will use the exposure.** That means emailing target coaches before every event with the bracket, field, time and jersey number. Our [guide to emailing college coaches](/how-to-email-college-soccer-coaches/) has the format. Without that step you are paying for a league and hoping.

**The schools on your list attend.** Check which programs came to those events in past years. If your athlete's realistic level is not represented, you are funding exposure to the wrong audience.

**It does not crowd out the rest of the plan.** If the fees mean no film, no camps and no time for outreach, the league is costing you recruiting rather than buying it.

## What I tell families who cannot afford it

You are not out of the running. Getting your name in front of coaches is the biggest thing, and that is free. Film costs nothing but your athlete's time. Emails cost nothing. A well-chosen ID camp at a school on your list costs less than a season of top-flight club fees and puts your kid in front of the coach who makes the decision.

This is also where I push families toward junior college and two years first. It is the most underused option in this whole conversation, and for a family stretched thin by club fees it does the same job the expensive league was supposed to do: real minutes, real development, and film that did not exist before. I played two years of junior college and transferred to a Division I program from there. That route cost my family a fraction of what the top-flight club circuit costs, and it worked.

The families who get recruited are not always the ones who spent the most. They are the ones who ran the process.

## FAQ

**Do you need ECNL to play college soccer?**
No. Those leagues concentrate college coaches in one place, which helps, but coaches recruit from lists built by athlete outreach. Plenty of recruited players come from other clubs.

**Is MLS Next worth the money?**
It is worth it if your athlete starts and plays real minutes, the schools on your list attend, and you use the events by emailing coaches beforehand. Without that last step you are paying for exposure you never collect.

**Will coaches ignore my athlete for playing in a smaller club?**
No. They will ignore an athlete they have never heard of, at any club. Film and direct outreach matter more than the league name.

**Is it better to start in a lower league or sit in a higher one?**
Start and play. Coaches cannot evaluate a player who is not on the field, and minutes against decent competition beat a bench role in a strong one.

**What should we spend on first if money is tight?**
Film, then targeted events where your specific list of schools attends, then the league.
