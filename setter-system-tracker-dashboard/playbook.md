## 1. Ground rules for every conversation

1. **Parents come first.** Parent DM conversations always get priority. Work an active athlete
   conversation only when no parent or other high-priority conversation is waiting. [source]
2. **Stay in a live conversation.** While a lead is replying or typing, don't leave to work other
   threads. Reply almost immediately. Don't let a live lead go cold. [source]
3. **Reply fast.** A lead's message gets an answer within **15 minutes** during working hours,
   and within **3 hours** outside them. Ifty and Mahbubal work from Egypt and the Middle East, so
   most hours are covered; there may be some blank hours overnight. [Izi, 2026-10-28]
4. **Follow up on time when a lead goes quiet** - see section 6 for the timing. [Izi, 2026-10-28]
5. **Follow the flow, every step, in order.** Reviewed DMs were skipping about half the steps.
   Skipping is the number one problem the training calls out. [source]
6. **The setter leads.** State the next step. Never hand the lead the decision about whether a
   call happens ("I'll drop my link", not "want me to send my link?"). [source]
7. **Use ChatGPT properly.** The playbook doc is added as a source in the setter's ChatGPT
   "setting" chat (since Oct 22; before that it was pasted in daily). Read the whole conversation
   first, screenshot it, steer ChatGPT ("we need his name", "already in college - just connect
   and close out"), then **reread before sending**. Never copy and paste a reply blind. [source]
8. **Reread every message before it goes out.** Cut repeated words, cut emoji stacks (three in a
   row is too many), fill in every placeholder. [source]
9. **No prices in DMs.** The program "is not something I can accurately quote or explain over
   DMs" - cost is for the call. [source]

## 2. Which flow applies

Pick the flow from who is talking and what they said. Using the wrong one is a mistake.

| Who it is | Flow |
|---|---|
| Parent of a 9th-12th grader | **Parent flow** (section 3) |
| Parent of a 5th-8th grader | **Junior Prep flow** (section 4) |
| The athlete themselves | **Athlete flow** (section 5) - move to a parent group chat |
| Engaged with content ("that resonated") | Content follow-up line, then into the parent flow at step 2 |
| Asking about ID camps | ID camp line, then into the parent flow at step 2 |
| Athlete is injured | Injury line, then into the parent flow at step 2 |
| Athlete already plays in college | No sales conversation. Connect and close out. [source] |
| University coach (NCAA D1/D2/D3, JUCO) | Coach line. Get school, boys or girls, name, Instagram handle; send to Coach Izi for the coach list. Check their bio if they keep it short. [source] |
| Non-soccer coach | Short thank-you line. [source] |
| Adult fan, no athlete | Short thank-you line. [source] |

The exact lines for each of these are in the "SETTING PLAYBOOK PTG" doc.

## 3. Parent flow (9th-12th grade) - the 7 steps

These are the stages the dashboard tracks. A conversation's "stage reached" is the last step it
completed properly.

### Step 1 - Inquiry
**Has to happen:**
- Thank them, ask for the athlete's **name**, grade and position. Parents usually give grade and
  position and skip the name. **Push for the name** - every later message uses it. [source]
- Then the current-picture question: training schedule, coaches outside team training, recruiting
  done so far (emails, school list, solo training). [source]
- This is a compound question, and people usually answer only the last part. **Circle back** to
  the parts they skipped before moving on. [source]

### Step 2 - Direction and Dig
**Has to happen:**
- Direction question: short-term development, or a long-term strategy that sets [name] up for
  college? [source]
- The Dig: "What are some challenges [name] is currently facing?" This **must be asked and must
  get an answer.** It's where the parent's real reason for reaching out comes out. [source]
- **Dig deeper on the answer** - don't just say thanks. "Tell me more about her confidence - when
  did you first notice it?" "Has the weak left foot always been there, or did it come after an
  injury?" [source]
- Done well: naming a pressure the parent hasn't said yet (balancing school, training and
  recruiting) and leaning in when they open up. [source]

### Step 3 - Harder Positioning Push
**Has to happen:**
- "Thank you for that insight - the good news is that's fixable..." naming **the exact issues the
  parent gave in the Dig**, then "...otherwise development can stall, leaving parents and athletes
  scrambling... starting way later than they should have." [source]
- This is the first message with **no question at the end**, on purpose. [source]
- Can be followed by "[name] is exactly the type of athlete I work with... Does that feel like
  where you're at right now?" [source]

### Step 4 - Authority Bridge
**Has to happen:**
- Explain what Izi does: long-term, individualized mentorship covering development, recruiting
  communication, exposure timing, scholarship positioning and decision-making. [source]
- Pick the version that fits the tone. **Higher authority** when the parent has been serious and
  formal. **Warmer** when the chat has been relaxed. [source]
- End with the micro-commitment: "Does that sound like the sort of long-term support you feel
  [he/she/name] would benefit from?" Use the real pronoun or name, never "he or she". [source]
- Why it can't be skipped: it ties back to their short vs long term answer, and it lets families
  who can't afford a serious program rule themselves out. "No more skipping this question." [source]

### Step 5 - Financial Qualification
**Has to happen:**
- Learn the family's real situation from **objective signals**, not just willingness. Asking
  "are you open to investing?" on its own only tells you they're willing if it's cheap enough.
  [source] Signals from the training:
  - club team - which level, and if not on one, why (money, level, distance)
  - private or extra coaching they already pay for
  - college fund, or relying on scholarships
  - what the parents do for work
  - how important scholarships are
- Then the qualifier: "That said, I only work with families who are fully committed... If it's the
  right fit, are you prepared to invest in outside support...?" [source]
- Decision-makers: "Because there is a financial investment involved, I ask that decision-makers
  join the call..." [source]
- **Families living outside the US:** confirm financial capacity early, using the signals above.
  It's a known trend of lower fit, and it affects time zones and US recruiting. It is **not** an
  automatic disqualifier. [Izi, 2026-10-27]
- **Never** score or qualify a family on heritage, ethnicity or the language the parents speak.
  A US-based family is qualified on the same signals as everyone else. [Izi, 2026-10-27]
- If they raise a cost concern, acknowledge exactly what they said and reframe it - for example,
  scholarships can make a big difference to the cost of college, which is a reason to start now.
  [source]

### Step 6 - Call Transition
**Has to happen:**
- **State** the call as the next step and drop the link in the same message. Short version
  (about 45 min Zoom, dive into [name]'s situation, "I'll drop my scheduling link rn!") or
  authority version for serious, high-level conversations ("not something I can accurately quote
  or explain over DMs... determine if [he/she] qualifies... I'll go ahead and drop my calendar
  link below"). [source]
- If the parent asks about cost or what a week looks like before this step, answer with what Izi
  would do **for this athlete**, looping back to details they shared earlier so it doesn't read
  copied. Don't jump straight to "book the call". [source]

### Step 7 - Close (getting the booking)
**Has to happen:**
- Right after the link: "Go ahead and grab a time... All decision-makers, including your athlete,
  are required to be on the call... Let me know what day and time you choose..." [source]
- **Follow up to confirm the booking either way.** Check the calendar (and the sales team
  WhatsApp channel where Ifty posts confirmed calls), then message them:
  - We see it: confirm the day and time back to them.
  - We don't: "Hey, I haven't seen your call on my calendar yet. Is it giving you any trouble?"
  [Izi, 2026-10-28; source]
- Not booked yet: follow the timing in section 6. If they need to check with mom or dad, agree a
  time to circle back, then follow up at that time. [source]
- **Wants the link but can't book yet** (e.g. waiting on a schedule or the other parent): this is
  its own group, kept separate from leads who just haven't booked. Agree when to check back.
  [Izi, 2026-10-28]
- **Before the call:** message them to confirm they'll attend. [Izi, 2026-10-28]
- **No-show:** after the scheduled time, reach out, check whether there was a miscommunication,
  and offer to set a new time. [Izi, 2026-10-28]

### Recovery - when the lead already wants the call early
Don't drop a lead who's ready to meet. Send the link, then ask the missed qualifying questions
straight after (what they've done to prepare for college, college fund, relying on scholarships,
ready for a high-commitment, high-investment program). [source]

## 4. Junior Prep flow (5th-8th grade)

**Steps:** [source]
1. "You're right on track reaching out early" - short-term (next six months) or longer-term
   plan heading into middle school or high school? Get the athlete's name.
2. Current training schedule, coaches outside team training, current challenges.
3. "What steps have you taken to help [name] overcome [issue]?"
4. Positioning statement - families at this stage add support to overcome [issue], otherwise
   development can stall, leaving the athlete discouraged and overwhelmed.
5. Then the same Authority Bridge, Financial Qualification, Call Transition and Close as the
   parent flow.

**Same direction, younger framing.** Every step runs in the same order as the high school flow,
but the messages are geared to a younger athlete: **development and growth, not college.**
Don't lead with recruiting, scholarships or college timelines with these families.
[Izi, 2026-10-28]

**What setters can say about how Junior Prep works** (from Coach Brittani's onboarding call, so
it's accurate):
- Built around the athlete and their week - Coach B finds the holes in their game, builds a plan
  and follows up. That's exactly what the parent in that call said they couldn't find from other
  trainers.
- Weekly 1:1 Zoom calls with Coach Brittani, plus a written recap after every call, sent to the
  athlete and the parent.
- Short weekly mental lessons in Skool (confidence, habits, leadership - small wins that build
  up).
- Assigned solo skill sessions scheduled around their team practices, filmed at the start for a
  baseline and again a few weeks later to show progress.
- Recovery and injury-prevention work where it's needed.
- An active Skool community with Izi, Coach B and the other athletes.

Never quote the price.

## 5. Athlete flow (athlete DMs directly)

The goal is to get the parents into a group chat quickly. Don't hold long conversations with
athletes. [source]

1. **Intro:** grade, position, long-term soccer goals.
2. **Development dig:** training schedule, what they most want to improve.
3. **Validate and position:** most athletes their age aren't training that intentionally; ask if
   they've talked with their parents about college soccer or a long-term plan.
4. **Confusion discovery** (if they don't know the process): that's normal, there's a lot of
   conflicting information, Izi builds a roadmap year by year.
5. **Transition to parents:** ask them to start a group chat with their parents, here on
   Instagram.
   - **Sandwich it:** pair the ask with a question so they stay engaged ("In the meantime, do you
     have any game footage?"). [source]
   - If they ignore the ask, **repeat it.** [source]
   - Fine to ask where they're from or whether they're in the US. [source]
6. **Group chat opener** as Izi (9th-12th or Junior Prep version from the doc), then the parent
   flow from step 2.

## 6. Follow-ups and recovering a quiet conversation

**Tone:** friendly and casual - never argumentative or pointed. GIFs and nice messages only.
[source]

**Timing when a lead goes quiet** (our message was the last one): [Izi, 2026-10-28]
1. First follow-up **15 minutes** after our message.
2. Second follow-up **1 hour** after that.
3. Third follow-up around the **3-hour mark**, before the end of the day.
4. **Next day:** follow-ups continue. [Izi, 2026-10-28] **[question]** How many, and at what
   times?

**Approved moves:** [source]
- A funny GIF, so they know a real person is there.
- "Let me know", "Keep me posted lol", "Checking in - what's the update?"
- Missing name, even late: "Also, what's his name?"
- Wanted to check in after learning more about [name] - "I'd love to see what kind of support
  you guys are looking for. Keep me posted."
- **"Maybe down the road":** offer a resource first - "Is [name]'s goal currently to play at a
  high level in college? I have some resources in mind if so." Push once more before the
  "whenever you're ready, feel free to reach back out" close.
- **Negative signal** (thumbs down, "depends on cost"): "Can I ask what changed?" **We work until
  they say no.**
- **Old leads:** go back and revive them. When an old lead replies to a story, use it to restart
  the conversation without selling (ask how old the athletes are, how the season is going).

**Banned - never send:** [source]
- "Hey, just wanted to be honest for a second. I've followed up a few times... my messages are
  being seen but not getting responses." Or anything like it. It's accusatory, it burns the lead,
  and athletes are often just at school.

## 7. Follow-up statuses the dashboard uses

| Status | Meaning | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| **Left on read by us** | The lead's message is the last one and we haven't answered. Most urgent. Late once it passes 15 minutes in working hours, 3 hours outside them. | Reply now, in the flow, at the step they're on |
| **Went quiet** | Our message is the last one and the lead stopped replying. | Follow-ups at 15 minutes, then 1 hour, then the 3-hour mark (section 6) |
| **Link sent - not booked** | Link sent, no booking confirmed | Booking follow-up (step 7), then the section 6 timing |
| **Has link - can't book yet** | Wants the link but can't book right now | Check back at the agreed time |
| **Booked** | Call is on the calendar | Confirm attendance before the call |
| **Booked - no show** | From GHL's "SALES TRACKING" pipeline | Reach out, check for a miscommunication, offer a new time |

## 8. Mistakes and impact levels

Impact levels approved by Izi on 2026-10-28 ("looks okay"). The behaviours themselves come from
the training.

### High - costs us the lead or a wasted call
| # | Mistake |
|---|---|
| H1 | Skipped a step before the booking link - most often the Dig, Authority Bridge or Financial Qualification |
| H2 | Rushed to "book the call" when the parent asked about cost, before the flow was done |
| H3 | Financial qualification was only "are you open to investing?", with no objective signals |
| H4 | Left a live conversation, or left a lead on read past 15 minutes (working hours) or 3 hours (outside them) |
| H5 | Sent the banned "seen but not responding" message, or any argumentative or guilt-tripping follow-up |
| H6 | Quoted a price in DMs |
| H7 | Scored or treated a US-based family differently because of heritage or language |

### Medium - weakens the conversation
| # | Mistake |
|---|---|
| M1 | Never got the athlete's name, or didn't use it after getting it |
| M2 | Compound question half-answered and the missing parts never circled back |
| M3 | The Dig was asked but not deepened - took the first answer and moved on |
| M4 | Positioning push was generic - didn't name the parent's own issues |
| M5 | Asked permission for the call ("would you like to book?") instead of stating it |
| M6 | Long athlete conversation without moving to a parent group chat, or the group chat ask wasn't repeated when ignored |
| M7 | A parent was left waiting while the setter worked an athlete conversation |
| M8 | Link sent without the decision-makers message, or no booking follow-up (see it / don't see it on the calendar) |
| M9 | Missed or late follow-ups when a lead went quiet (15 minutes, 1 hour, 3-hour mark) |
| M13 | No attendance confirmation before a booked call, or no outreach after a no-show |
| M14 | Junior Prep family pitched on college and recruiting instead of development and growth |
| M10 | Gave up before a clear no - closed out "maybe down the road" without the resource offer and one more push, or didn't ask "what changed?" |
| M11 | Wrong flow for the lead (e.g. 9th-12th grade script to a 6th grader, sales flow to a college player or coach) |
| M12 | Ran a sales conversation with a family outside the US without confirming financial capacity early |

### Low - polish
| # | Mistake |
|---|---|
| L1 | Placeholder or template text left in ("he or she", "(ATHLETE NAME)") |
| L2 | Emoji stacks, repeated words, obvious copy-paste |
| L3 | Didn't loop back to details the lead shared earlier, so the reply reads like a template |
| L4 | Wrong authority bridge version for the tone of the conversation |

## 9. How the audit uses this (for the build)

For each conversation, the audit records: the setter, the account, the flow used, the stage
reached (section 3, 4 or 5), each mistake with the line that was sent, what the flow calls for,
the impact level and a link to the DM, plus the follow-up status from section 7.

**No percentage score.** Each conversation and each setter is measured by the **number of
mistakes, split by severity** (high, medium, low), with the high ones highlighted.
[Izi, 2026-10-28] Only steps the conversation actually reached count - a lead that went quiet at
step 2 isn't marked down for steps 3-7.

Anything the playbook doesn't cover is flagged as a question for Izi, never as a mistake.
