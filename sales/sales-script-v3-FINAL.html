Soccer Recruitment 101 - Strategy Session Script v3 FINAL (Oct 2026)

Internal only. Have this open in split screen on every Strategy Session.

Built from 55 recorded calls, January to September 2026:

  • 41 closes and 14 non-closes in total.
  • From "sales script 101 - v2 (Sept 2026)": 10 closes and 8 non-closes, June to September.
  • From the January, March and April review: 23 closes and 6 lost calls.

The evidence behind every line, with the family name and timestamp, is in sales/sales-call-playbook.md and sales/sales-script-v3-master.md.

Decisions made in this version - change any you disagree with

  1. Coaching structure. Described as weekly 1:1 calls and "you stay in until you sign." No fixed number of months. The calls said both "6 months of weekly calls" and "weekly, then every other week, then monthly."
  2. Payment ladder. Taken from v2, the most recent: card up front, then half and half, then 3 payments of $2,666, then external financing. The one-off plans from the spring calls are dropped: 4 x $2,100, 6 x $1,460 and $1,500 down.
  3. Guarantee. Stated once, plainly, inside the offer, not held back until asked. The exact terms still need your confirmation (Part 5).
  4. Commitment scale. Keep the requirement of a 10, drop the judgment. No "that's very low" and no "I wouldn't let you in."
  5. Trust objection. "You want to make sure you're not getting scammed - is that what I'm hearing?" replaces "You think I'm going to scam you?"
  6. Which program, based on the calls that closed. Please confirm.
    • Junior Prep for younger athletes who need development first. On the closes this was 7th and 8th graders and an early freshman.
    • Recruitment 101 for athletes in or nearing the recruiting window.
    • The $4,500 option for the right athlete when $8,000 isn't possible.

Timing

  • Discovery done by about 25 minutes.
  • Offer at 30-35 minutes.
  • Price between 31 and 47 minutes.
  • Call done by 50-60 minutes.

The two longest lost calls ran 72 minutes, with the price at minute 63-64.

Step 0 - Before the call

  1. Booking message: "Looking forward to our call. One thing I ask of every family: both parents (or whoever helps make decisions for [athlete]) need to be on, on camera, with [athlete]. If that time doesn't work for everyone, message me and we'll move it."
  2. Write every name on a sticky note: the athlete, each parent, the club, the school.
  3. Pull their grade, GPA, club and league from the form, so you confirm instead of ask.

Step 1 - Open (0-3 min)

  1. "Can you hear me and see me okay?"
  2. "Tell me how to say your name so I don't embarrass myself."
  3. "Where are you guys calling in from?"
    • Find one real personal tie: club, hometown, a mutual player, homeschool, faith.
    • With a faith family: "Do you mind if I just pray for us really quick?"
  4. "And then [grade] with a [GPA], correct?" Praise what's real.
  5. "Right now still a good time? You'll have about 45 minutes?"

Decision-maker check - every call, before the frame

  1. "Before we jump in - does anyone else need to be on the call who's going to be part of making a decision, that it would be helpful for them to hear what it looks like?"
  2. If someone is missing:
    • Ask: "Does [mom/dad] want to pop in so they get the overview too? I want to not have to relay the information twice."
    • If they can't join now: "Can we get them on by phone for the last 15 minutes?"
    • If they can't join at all: run discovery, then book the offer call with both of them before you pitch.
  3. If the athlete is missing: "[Athlete] is gonna need to be on the call, for sure."

Step 2 - Frame (by min 3)

"What I want to do, guys, is deep dive on your situation - where you're at, what you're struggling with and where you need the most help. As long as I know for a fact I can help you, I'll offer you a spot in the program and show you how it works. If I don't feel like we're the right fit, I'll just point you in the best direction I see fit for your situation. Does that sound good?"

Step 3 - Why we're here (3-6 min)

  1. "[Athlete], why don't you start by telling me - what's the main reason we're on the call today?"
  2. "Parents, what do you guys see? I want to hear your perspective, it's very valuable here."
  3. "What made you book the call?"
  4. "How long have you been wanting to play college soccer?"

Step 4 - Where they're at (6-15 min)

Recruitment

  1. "Catch me up - what have you guys done as far as recruitment, if anything?"
  2. "How many schools have you emailed? How often?"
  3. "Are you sending film? Do we have a highlight reel? Is it on YouTube?"
  4. "Do you have a soccer Instagram? How often do you post on it?"
  5. "When you say you've talked to coaches - have you had actual phone calls, or is it all emails?"
  6. "Have we developed any relationship with any of these programs? Tell me about that."
  7. Juniors and up: "On June 15th and the days after, did any school reach out saying they want to talk?"
  8. "What are your strongest leads right now?"
  9. "Any ID camps or showcases coming up this summer?"

Training and the athlete

  1. "What does your training week look like? Give me the full picture." Then confirm: "So we're on the ball about six days a week?"
  2. "How many days are you on the ball outside team training?"
  3. "Any weight training or strength work?"
  4. "What did breakfast look like today?" / "Have you ever tracked your grams of protein?"
  5. "High school - JV or varsity? Club - what level? What position?"
  6. "Any injuries?"
  7. "What's the biggest weakness in your game right now?"
  8. "Do you know what you want to major in? What inspired that?"

Other options - ask here, not after the price

"Have you guys looked at or worked with any other recruiting services? What did you think?"

Step 5 - The dream (15-20 min)

  1. "What's the dream? Tell me what your dream is - not what you think you can do."
  2. "What makes this a successful outcome for you? Division, distance, academics, major?" If they light up: "You started smiling when you talked about that. Tell me more."
  3. "Why D1? Tell me more about that." / "How long has that been a dream of yours?"
  4. "Location, climate, distance from home - where are you willing to go, and where are you not?"
  5. "If you found a program that was the right fit - coach, environment, major, you felt supported - would that still be a win, even if it wasn't [D1/dream school]?"
  6. To the parents: "Anything that's like, this has to be here for this to make sense for us?"

Step 6 - The gap: let them name it (20-27 min)

  1. "What do you guys feel is the biggest obstacle between where you are now and having those offers?" Ask the athlete, then the parents.
  2. "We do not have any offers on the table currently, correct? Why do you guys think that is?"
  3. "Is it the time that's hard, or the plan and the what-to-do that's hard?"
  4. "Are you guys feeling confident navigating this on your own?" / "Do you feel like we have a solid recruitment strategy right now?"
  5. "What's your plan right now to fix that?"
  6. "Where's that belief block coming from?" then "When your confidence drops, what actually happens?" Go two layers deep.
  7. "Do you guys have a plan for the next four weeks before [their camp/date]?"
  8. To the parent: "What is your biggest fear with where [athlete] is at right now?" Then the inverse: "How would you feel knowing you've got a plan and a path?"
  9. Cost of waiting: "If the next six months look like the last six, where does that leave you on June 15th?"
  10. Urgency from the real calendar: June 15th, their showcases, and "Eighth grade is really ninth grade, especially on the girls' side."
  11. Coach on the call: "Can you guys allow me to put on my coaching hat for a second here and be a coach?" Then give them the honest route.
    • Or the hard truth with a deadline: "I'm going to be brutally honest with you guys because I want to help you get where you want to go..."
    • If status is driving the dream, use the forced choice: D1 and riding the bench, or D2/NAIA and playing - "Which one are you choosing?"
    • If they say talking to coaches is the problem: "You're talking to me right now, right? And I'm a coach."
  12. Willingness: "What are you willing to do to get to that level?" If the route may be JUCO or a lower level first: "Is that something we're willing to do, if push comes to shove?"
  13. Label it back: "So right now you're dealing with [X], you want [goal] by [date], and what's missing is the blueprint and the accountability. Would you say that's accurate? Anything I'm missing?" Wait for "that's accurate."
    • If they say they're already doing a lot: "You've built the machine. What we do is make it consistent and targeted."
    • Never agree that they don't need you.

Step 7 - What it would mean (27-30 min)

  1. "[Athlete], what would it mean to you to find the right fit and get a scholarship to keep playing?"
  2. "[Parent], what would it mean to you?" Ask each parent separately.
  3. The quiet parent, directly: "[Name], you've been quiet over there - what's important to you in this?"
  4. "How would it feel to actually have a plan this time around?"
  5. If scholarship money matters: "I have a kid who just got $19,000 a year - almost $80,000. What would that mean to you guys?"
  6. "What is the number one thing that needs to be in a program like this for it to be a win for you guys?" Build the offer around their answer.
  7. If the athlete's answer is soft: "I don't hear much conviction yet, and that's okay - I just need to know this is something you really want, not something you feel you have to do."

Step 8 - Money, decisions and the right program (before the pitch)

  1. "What's your guys' plan right now to pay for college?"
  2. If scholarship money matters, use real tuition and average-scholarship numbers, then: "Do we have a path to pay for that?"
  3. "When you invest in something like this for [athlete], how do you two usually make the decision?"
  4. If scholarships aren't the goal, sell development and fit, not the dollar math.
  5. Pick the program now:
    • Junior Prep for younger athletes who need development first.
    • Recruitment 101 for athletes in or nearing the window.
    • The $4,500 option for the right athlete when $8,000 isn't possible.
    Quote the next program one way only.

Step 9 - Commitment, before the pitch

  1. "Last thing I need, [athlete]. Not where you are right now - how committed are you to doing the work this takes? One is no way, 10 is 100% all in."
  2. If 10: "Good answer. I needed to hear that from you."
  3. If under 10:
    • "What would make it a 10?" or "Tell me where that hesitation is - is it the fear of what it entails, or you're not sure it's what you want?"
    • Then: "I need to know this is a 10 for you before I offer you a spot."
  4. "And parents, you'd say that's for sure as well?"
  5. "Would you be willing to show up to a weekly call and roll out an action plan every week? And you're not going to join and then ghost us?"

Step 10 - The offer (30-40 min)

  1. "Guys, I can tell 100% you're the right fit. I'd love to work with you. Let me walk you through how it works - if you have questions, raise your hand, holler at me. Sound good?"
  2. Story: match it to their situation - injury, homeschool, late start, no big club, keeper, JUCO. Recruited five times, JUCO to D1. Keep it short.
  3. Pillar 1, Self-Promotion: the World Cup player, "be your best promoter." Tie it to something they told you. Then "Does that make sense?"
  4. Pillar 2, Sellability: "coaches are essentially purchasing you," film, personal statement video, "person over player." Use the money math only if Step 8 said scholarships matter. Then "Does that make sense?"
  5. Pillar 3, Outreach: "we write every single email," volume, consistency, follow-up, a four-week rotation. Then "Does that make sense?"
  6. Structure: weekly 1:1 calls, parent recaps, DM access, the community, parents only on decision calls, and you stay in until you sign.
  7. Proof: screen-share the Skool community - real families, real wins. "That's my reference for you."
  8. Guarantee: state it once, plainly, the same words every time. [INSERT CONFIRMED TERMS - Part 5]
  9. Solve one live problem before the price. Draft the film email to their club, review their Instagram, or coach the ID camp plan.
  10. "I went fast. What questions do you guys have?" Then: "[Athlete], any questions?"

Step 11 - Next steps, then the price

  1. Before the number: "From here it would just be sending over the payment link, getting that taken care of, then sending you an onboarding email and booking our first meeting for [day]."
  2. "You guys want to hear what it takes to get started?" Wait for "Yes."
  3. "The investment is $8,000. You stay in until you get the result you want." Stop talking.
  4. "Is that something you're able to do?"
  5. If they ask what's included: "Start to sign. Nothing else. That's it."

Payment order

  1. "Most of my families put that on a credit card up front. Is that something you're able to do?"
  2. "If that's tight, half down and half in 30 days."
  3. "Or three monthly payments of $2,666."
  4. External financing, 6-24 months: "Where would you say your credit is - good, fair, excellent?"

When they ask for a longer monthly plan: "Honestly, the heavy work is in the first month or two, so the most I break it into is half now, half in 30 days."

Step 12 - Close

  1. "Is that something we want to move forward with?" / "Sounds like we want to do this - is that what I'm hearing?" / "You guys want to get started?"
  2. "You got the card on hand you want to use?"
  3. Onboard live while they pay, every time:
    • Athlete email in the chat.
    • Payment link in the chat or by message.
    • Agreement.
    • Skool join and profile picture.
    • Tag them in the intro video.
    • Coach Brittani intro.
    • First weekly call booked within 1-3 days ("What time do you go to school?").
    • Homework for today.
  4. Call back their commitment: "There you go. Commitment. That's why I asked you."
  5. If the athlete wobbles after the yes, slow down and talk it through. Without a plan they end up with one offer and loans.

Part 3 - Objections

"We need to talk it over" / "I'll discuss with my wife"

  1. "Tell me, what's the biggest reservation for you right now?"
  2. "So the biggest thing is the financial commitment, correct?"
  3. "If I could break that down for you, would you be comfortable moving forward?" Then offer half and half.
  4. "Do you think you're doing enough right now to be in that top 1% and stand out to these coaches?"
  5. "I feel like we're right there. What's the hesitation for us right now?" / "What do you need help thinking about?"
  6. "Within 24 hours we forget about half of what was discussed. Would it be okay if we just move forward with that first payment so we could get everything started on our end?"

The spouse isn't on the call

  1. Get them on now: "Do you want to call him real quick and see if we can patch him back on?"
  2. "Totally fair. What do you feel [spouse] needs to hear to get to the same yes you're at?"
  3. "Let's grab 15 minutes tomorrow with both of you and I'll answer their questions directly. What time works?" Book it before you hang up.
  4. Send a short written recap of the plan that night.

"That's a lot"

  1. "How are you feeling about that?"
  2. "Is that more than you were expecting, or more than you're able to do?"
  3. "What's the plan to pay for four years of college?"
  4. "If someone said, give me $8,000 and I'll give you $90,000, you'd probably take the deal."
  5. "What we can't afford to do is wait. That ends up costing a lot more."
  6. Change the payment structure, never the price.

"We've been burned before" / trust

  1. "Where have you been burned before? Expand on that for me."
  2. "You want to make sure you're not getting scammed - is that what I'm hearing?"
  3. "I appreciate you keeping it real with me. Let me show you my community." Then screen-share.
  4. "It's not about the money. It's about the investment. It's the fact that it stings - because then I've got a serious commitment from you."

"Is it worth it?" / "Will this actually work for her?"

  1. The guarantee, stated plainly. Don't argue the number.
  2. "Where would you project her?" Give an honest level with a contrast: "I told a family this morning NAIA. For her, 100% Division I."

"What if she ends up at [the school we already know] anyway?"

"That's a very risky move - all the eggs in one basket. You never know until you've had the experience and the offers are on the table."

"How is this different from NCSA?"

"A lot of our students actually come from NCSA. They do lots of sports; we only do soccer. The difference is the one-to-one - you're not waiting an hour on a Zoom, you just message me."

The athlete isn't driving it

  1. Agree with the parent, then turn to the athlete with the 1-10. Let the athlete answer the parent's worry.
  2. "They're going to hear it better from someone else. It's nothing on you."

"Is this too early?"

Offer Junior Prep or the $4,500 option with an upgrade path. Don't give them "too early" as a reason to wait.

They still won't decide today

  1. "When do you guys think you'll be able to sit down as a family and talk about it?"
  2. "So when should I put it on my end to check in with you, to make sure nothing falls through the cracks on my end?" Move the existing invite to that time, with every decision-maker on it.
  3. If money isn't the issue and it's a first meeting, don't push for a decision in 20 minutes. Book the follow-up instead.
  4. Never end on "if I don't hear from you by X."
    • Every deferred close from January to April ended with a booked day and time.
    • Every lost call ended without one.

Part 4 - Stop doing

  • Pitching with a decision-maker missing.
  • Letting the call run long.
  • "That's incredibly low" / "I wouldn't let you in" on the 1-10.
  • "I feel like you don't want that" and "So you think it's a scam?"
  • "Go look around, go see." Say instead: "Who else are you looking at? Let's compare right now."
  • Agreeing that a warm family doesn't need you, or disqualifying them. Offer the right-sized option.
  • Pitching $8,000 to an athlete you just told is early.
  • Quoting the next program different ways.
  • Having no answer to "what's your success rate?"
  • Letting a competitor sit unaddressed.
  • Asking a first-meeting family to decide in 20 minutes when money isn't the issue.
  • Refusing a written summary.
  • Holding back the guarantee, or changing its terms mid-sentence.
  • Different numbers for the same thing.
  • Getting names wrong.
  • Saying the price while a decision-maker isn't paying attention.
  • Ending without a booked next step.

Part 5 - Confirm before these go back into the script

Each of these was said on recorded calls, in different words. Pick one version you can back up.

  1. The guarantee - exact terms. Versions on record:
    • Money back at year end if no scholarship.
    • A scholarship never less than the investment.
    • "I guarantee scholarship back in investment."
    • "At least five offers... I don't guarantee this part yet."
    • "No refund if you quit halfway."
    • "I do offer a money back guarantee, but I don't even like to bring that up."
    • It is in the agreement.
  2. "100% success rate." Sometimes explained as "I only take committed families."
  3. "Average about five offers." One parent pushed back: "I don't know anybody that's gotten five offers this year."
  4. Student count: 93 (June), 170 (July), about 25 active, and "27 now, capped around 30... about 90-92 in the full program" (April).
  5. "Six weeks" average time to offers (from an ad).
  6. "We're the only program that offers a money-back guarantee."
  7. "99% re-sign" on Junior Prep.
  8. The podcast invite - is it still on offer?
