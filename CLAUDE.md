# Push the Goal Soccer - working context

Read this at the start of every session. It replaces re-explaining the business.

## The business

Izi Chinchay (also appears as Izi Murray on Skool) coaches high school soccer players
through the college recruitment process. Clients are the athlete plus their parents,
usually together. Revenue comes from a small number of high-ticket coaching packages,
plus an Instagram-led lead magnet funnel that feeds strategy calls.

The two recurring jobs are:
1. Turning a recorded call into written follow-up that goes to the athlete and the parent.
2. Turning a signed deal into an onboarding package and a handoff to the coach.

## Voice - the thing that matters most

Direct, intense, no-BS, authoritative. It should read like coaching, not like impressing
anyone. Real experience, hard truths, clear action steps. Everything points back to
discipline, ownership and the long game. Faith and purpose sit underneath where they fit
naturally.

**Never:** motivational fluff, LinkedIn or corporate tone, clichés, talking down,
over-explaining, emoji-heavy copy, sarcasm, ultimatums.

**Mechanics:** no em-dashes - use spaced hyphens. Write "message", not "text". Say
"athlete" or "student", never "child". No emojis in body copy. (The one exception:
lead magnet email subject lines each open with a single relevant emoji.)

### The warmth correction (2026-08-21) - overrides the above where they collide

Izi's words after reviewing a first pass: *"I need it to be a little bit nicer. Some of
this comes off a little bit mean. I want it to be a little bit more positive."*

This is not a request for soft or generic. Keep the directness, the specifics, the honest
assessment and the real deadlines. What changes is the frame: a coach who is genuinely
excited about this kid and pushing them forward, not one auditing their failures.

The failure mode is an accurate, well-organised message where every paragraph is something
the kid did not do. Each line is defensible alone; the accumulation is what stings. Izi
describes himself as "a coach who actually cares but refuses to let you stay average" -
the caring half has to be visible on the page.

Concrete rules that follow from it:
- Open with a specific, real win. Never open with the problem.
- Frame gaps as the next step, not the failure.
- Assume good intent. A kid who goes quiet is unsure, not lazy - say so out loud.
- Pair every hard truth with why it works in their favour, and leave a door open.
- Never write a line implying the athlete is in debt to Izi. "what you owe me" becomes
  "what we need done". Same side of the table.
- Close warm. "Chat soon", not "don't ghost me".

For parent messages specifically: lead with progress, give the kid real credit, and do
**not** include a paragraph cataloguing what the kid has failed to do. That was the single
biggest thing Izi cut from a draft. Give one or two concrete ways to help. Thank them.
Sign "Izi".

Full detail with before/after examples: `skills/push-the-goal-call-notes/references/voice.md`

## Hard rules

- **Never send anything.** Every message - athlete, parent, coach - is left as an unsent
  draft, and the thread is marked unread so it shows blue. Izi reviews and sends himself,
  every time. He almost always makes small edits, and that is the point.
- **Once he has sent it, it is finished.** Tone feedback given after a send applies to the
  *next* athlete, not to rewriting what already went out.
- **Ground everything in the transcript.** Never invent schools, GPAs, stats, or recruiting
  timeline claims. A wrong claim in a parent message is worse than a missing one.
- **Keep sensitive health or personal details out** of PDFs and out of memory. Reference the
  downstream need without naming a diagnosis.
- **One game plan per athlete, one link.** Build and host it once. The coach's Skool DM and the
  tracker row carry the identical URL. Never make a second copy so something has a link to
  point at - two copies drift, and the coach and the tracker end up showing different plans for
  the same kid.
- **Don't make him babysit.** Run the whole workflow end to end without stopping to confirm
  each step. Ask only when a real fork exists.

## Tooling notes that keep biting

- **Fathom:** filter meetings by date window only. Do not filter by recorder or email, it
  misses meetings. Call titles are inconsistent, so match on last name too. Speaker labels
  are frequently scrambled on phone calls - read for content, not labels. Calls sometimes
  cut off mid-conversation; build from what was recorded and flag the gap.
- **Skool:** plain Enter **sends**. Use `shift+Return` for line breaks, twice for a blank
  line. Never overwrite an existing unsent draft. Attach files with `file_upload`, never the
  paperclip. Mark threads unread **last**, since reopening marks them read again.
- **Google Sheets:** enter values one cell per `type` action with a separate `key` "Tab"
  between them. Never embed a tab character inside a `type` string - it jams the whole row
  into column A.
